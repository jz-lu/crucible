CRUCIBLE — A PROPOSED STRUCTURE FOR RESEARCH THAT EVOLVES
6 November 2026 · design proposal, not an implemented or deployed workflow

Recommendation

Keep notes as the source of research. Let a note optionally contain precisely
addressable statements and arguments. Record which exact argument Crucible has
adopted for each scheme property or attack result. Preserve the existing reader
and review center as the human interfaces.

The participant should mainly encounter three familiar things: a note, a
statement, and an update proposal. Definitions, dependency uses and adoption
records supply precision underneath; they need not become separate dashboards.

Options considered

A. Annotated ordinary notes
   Assumptions remain pinned passages, with relationships described in prose.
   Best for: unrestricted exploration and a small editorial research collection.
   Advantage: almost no new authoring machinery; existing citations do the work.
   Cost: determining identity, affected uses and required bounds remains manual.
   Verdict: preserve this mode for ordinary research. It is insufficient by
   itself for the dependable update workflow requested here.

B. Canonical statements inside notes — recommended
   An optional statement block supplies its exact definition once. Arguments
   reference such blocks; reviewed selections determine the current presentation.
   Best for: notebook-first research with precise reuse and lifecycle tracking.
   Advantage: the card, note and machine interface share a definition. No second
   independent research document needs to be maintained.
   Cost: a small authoring/API contract and persistent adoption records are needed.
   Verdict: the best balance for Crucible's current direction.

C. Independent assumption records, explained by notes
   Definitions have their own authoritative records and versions. Notes provide
   explanation, proofs and criticism around them.
   Best for: a large independently curated collection with separate ownership,
   alias management and definition governance.
   Advantage: strong independent reuse; ownership need not follow one note.
   Cost: authors must understand whether to change the definition or its note.
   Research acquires a second source of authority and a second editing surface.
   Verdict: a credible later direction if governance demands it. It does not
   require a global assumptions page, but that is not its main tradeoff here.

What belongs inside a note

Full text remains the free-form technical record. The reader-facing companion
remains optional. Neither ordinary publication nor research participation
requires a companion, assumption block, fixed outline or special note type.

A statement block is optional structure, like a numbered theorem inside a paper:
  - stable identity and a short title;
  - the exact mathematical statement;
  - pinned definitions needed to interpret it, where applicable;
  - a mathematical category, distinct from an assessment of truth.

It can define a hardness conjecture, a probability inequality or a modeling
premise. Its complete definition includes the distribution, parameter domain,
quantifiers, goal and model, whether inline or in pinned normative references.
Do not force every claim into a rigid collection of formula fields; arbitrary
mathematics remains text plus exact references.

The same block renders in the note and its compact card. Explanation surrounds
or links to it, rather than manually copying its definition. A full-text-only
note can still expose a statement through a generated formal view. It does not
need an extra human-written article. Existing section and line citations remain
valid; generated blocks must not be inserted into historical stored body text.

An assumption is a role, not a permanent verdict

A statement can be assumed in one argument and justified in another. A proof
does not require deleting its A-number or replacing its original definition
with a theorem-shaped rewrite. Keep identity, evidence and current use separate.

An argument records a conclusion and the premises it uses, with scope and an
exact note location for the reasoning. The conclusion can be the security or
correctness claim for a scheme release, an attack's success/work account, or
another mathematical statement. This need not create another top-level page.

Within an argument, the listed premises are required together. Different
arguments can independently justify the same conclusion:

  Claim C
    Argument 1 uses A and B
    Argument 2 uses D

If A fails, Argument 1 loses that justification. Argument 2 is unaffected unless
the evidence also addresses it. Choose one argument for the main presentation;
make alternatives quietly reachable when they exist. Do not flatten all three
premises into one list or build a general Boolean proof engine.

A use is more precise than a citation

Each use connects an argument to an exact statement definition and identifies:
  - parameter substitution and actual target distribution;
  - configuration coverage using stable IDs, with stages as display labels;
  - the required threshold or strength;
  - the exact justification for applying it in this setting.

For example: a named attack requires native-message decoding failure <=1/8
for particular configurations. A stronger key-agreement claim has a different
event/bound. Sharing a title or A-label cannot make evidence transferable.
Symbolic hardness statements with unspecified epsilon are still useful, but
must not silently become numerical security claims.

An empty dependency list is not evidence of an unconditional result. Distinguish
"dependencies not recorded" from a reviewed argument establishing its conclusion
without unproved premises, within its stated model and scope. Likewise, an
estimated attack-work curve is not a lower bound on security against all attacks.
Keep these meanings explicit in the result metadata and its focused reader.

Reading layout options

1. Inline expansion beneath the graph
   Fast for one short statement, but long mathematics makes the graph page tall
   and duplicates information. Keep compact previews, not the full workflow here.

2. A side panel retaining the graph
   Good spatial continuity, but cramped for proofs and requires a different
   mobile design. Useful only if later testing shows frequent short comparisons.

3. The existing focused note reader — recommended
   Spacious mathematics, one familiar reading surface, exact source context.
   Requires reliable back navigation preserving the selected point and scroll.

Proposed reader hierarchy

  A-12   [Short mathematical title]
  [Canonical formal statement]

  Used here: [the selected result, coverage and required strength]
  [Optional explanation, followed by its full technical source when requested]

  Related research
    [Proof/support/challenge notes, attributed and precisely targeted]
    [Any scoped assessment, with its reason and evidence]

  Other uses                         History
  Propose update

This is an information hierarchy, not five permanent tabs. Omit empty secondary
sections. A normal visit to the note has no artificial "Used here" strip; that
context arrives when following a scheme or curve point. Exact citations never
depend on the transient navigation context. Full text stays one action away
whether or not a readable companion exists. When absent, a short notice states
that no readable explanation has been added. Exact Full-text passage links still
open the cited passage directly.

Keep the current scheme Security assumptions / Correctness assumptions buttons
and point-specific Attack specification plus A-labels. The overview remains
quiet. The main reading view answers the more detailed questions.

Contributing research versus proposing a change

Publishing a proof, objection or experiment does not require an update proposal.
Notes can cite an exact statement/use and say supports, challenges, reproduces
or responds to. These are attributed research relationships, not verdicts.

Use "Propose update" when asking Crucible to change its adopted presentation.
Starting from a note, premise or claim prefills the exact context. Ask for:
  Evidence — an existing note, or a new note to write.
  Requested change — what argument, bound or assessment should be replaced.
  Applies to — exact uses/configurations, with a clear preview.

Example preview:
  "Use this proof for the enumeration attack at these configurations.
   Its required failure bound is satisfied. The scheme's stronger correctness
   claim and other configurations are not part of this update."

There is no separate prove/refute/replace portal. Do not require the participant
to retype IDs or copy information from a browser into an agent prompt. The agent
obtains the same exact context through the API and submits the same proposal.
If publication and proposing are combined in one explicit author action, their
durable states remain separate: screening is not scientific review.

Review should feel like reviewing a concrete change

  WHAT CHANGES       Current argument/definition -> proposed argument/definition
  WHY                Exact evidence, reproduction, remaining premises
  IMPACT             Uses, configurations, alternatives and release work

Use the existing review center. For an explicitly scoped, validated display-only
update, one "Approve and apply" action may atomically record the assessment and
the adoption. Separate records do not require two human clicks.

This is a new, deliberately authorized operation; existing historical
"recommend" assessments must not suddenly acquire application authority.
An update to executable algorithms, parameters or challenge policy instead
becomes approved/awaiting release, with the ordinary tested deployment process.
Scientific approval never executes code embedded in a note.

Application rechecks exact source pins, visibility, reviewer authority and the
expected current adoption event/version. Use that event identity, not only a
content hash, so a revert/reapply sequence cannot make a stale proposal current.
An outdated proposal gets a clear comparison/refresh path, not a silent overwrite.
Retries produce the same committed effect. No automatic changes to scores.

Version and ownership policy

* Every saved note edit creates a new note revision, as now.
* Changing normative definition text or its pinned definitions creates a new
  statement version. Do not infer mathematical equivalence automatically.
* Changes confined to titles/explanation leave the definition identity intact,
  while still creating a new note revision. Old citation routes stay exact.
* Existing uses stay pinned until a reviewed update selects a replacement.
  "New version available" does not mean better, accepted or used everywhere.
* A different author can propose an alternative from their own note. They do
  not overwrite the first author's statement or need that author's permission
  to publish criticism. Copies do not acquire identity by copying an A-label.
* Retraction, source unavailability and mathematical refutation are different.
  A withdrawn/private source must not leak through cached text or reverse links.

Lifecycle rehearsals

Case 1 — a weaker proof is useful
  A proof establishes delta_m<=2^-100 where an attack needs <=1/8. Review can
  adopt it for the attack. It neither refutes nor establishes a stronger
  key-agreement guarantee such as delta_K<=2^-128. The events differ too.

Case 2 — a scoped contradiction
  Evidence establishes an actual contradiction for stated parameters. The
  proposed update identifies the affected argument and alternatives. A single
  failure is not automatically a refutation of a probabilistic guarantee.
  Accepted evidence changes the current assessment with a precise explanation;
  it does not automatically declare the whole scheme broken or erase history.

Case 3 — a proof uses another premise
  A follows from B. Show a conditional justification and keep B discoverable.
  The reviewer identifies the remaining dependency set. A-from-B-from-A does
  not discharge either premise. No automatic theorem composition is needed.

Case 4 — one argument fails, another survives
  The selected proof uses A; an independent accepted argument uses D. Review
  can select the latter if its scope fits. Do not automatically switch methods,
  merge dependencies or claim the conclusion false because the first proof failed.

Case 5 — a definition changes or another researcher offers a replacement
  Compare exact versions, parameter maps and affected uses. Adopt for selected
  uses only. An abandoned author cannot block progress; attribution and original
  definitions survive. Older curves remain reproducible and clearly historical.

Case 6 — an active round overlaps an update
  An evidence-only change does not silently alter the instance, key, deadline
  or accepted submission. Malformed/unverifiable instances need an explicit
  operational decision. Changed algorithms/parameters get new release identities.

All these are workshop scenarios, not claimed new scientific discoveries.

Implementation shape and order

1. Add an optional canonical statement-block contract to note revisions. Render
   both card and note from it; preserve old notes and their hashes. Supporting
   ordinary notes require no conversion. Provide a compact human authoring
   affordance and an equivalent structured agent representation.
2. Add exact argument/dependency-use references, including cross-note reuse.
   Build a small derived index for visible related evidence and "Used by".
   Include two uses of one premise and two alternative arguments for one claim.
3. Add explicit update/adoption records through the existing review path, with
   scoped impact previews and concurrency/idempotency checks. Do not treat the
   local fixture manifest or its illustrative publication decisions as admission.
4. Rehearse the cases through real local APIs and UI. Keep the scenarios separate
   from actual research notes. Test withdrawal/privacy and conflicting updates.
5. Prepare production migration, compatibility, storage/screening bounds and
   release checks only after this workflow is usable. No extra service, graph
   database or LLM-based mathematical adjudicator is needed for the first version.

Defer: global catalogue navigation, automatic semantic merging, automatic proof
composition or argument fallback, an interactive universal dependency graph,
new reward rules, and splitting human/model text into separate publication units.
