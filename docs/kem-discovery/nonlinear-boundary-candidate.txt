Retained candidate: nonlinear boundary actions
Research record, 14 October 2026

This investigation followed docs/kem-discovery-agent-prompt.txt, then changed
scope when the user requested construction, candidate preservation, and no
concerted attacks. The result is an explicit nonlinear candidate with an exact
correctness argument and reproducible experiments. It is not a validated KEM,
and a new source of average-case hardness has not been established. Retain it
as a research lead in polynomial actions, with its relationship to existing
assumptions unresolved.

There is a concrete improvement in the construction: honest parties can agree
without expanding their entire secret transformations or computing canonical
equations for an algebraic variety. They exchange unordered samples of boundary
data, transform the samples pointwise, and sort them before deriving a key.
Only the public generators need polynomial expansion. This is a representation
and correctness result, not evidence of secrecy.

The first investigation concerned linear network responses. Before the scope
change, it found a weakness in that baseline and produced
network-subspace-results.json and ../../scripts/kem_discovery_networks.py.
Those historical artifacts are preserved, including their original status.
That work was not extended or rerun after the scope change. The current script,
../../scripts/kem_discovery_nonlinear.py, contains construction and correctness
checks only. Neither candidate has been registered in Crucible's catalogue.

The mathematical starting point was the response of a hidden electrical
network. For a boundary/interior decomposition of its grounded matrix,

  Q = [ A  B ; B^T  D ],       R = A - B D^{-1} B^T,

the boundary retains R while many internal representations may be forgotten.
This is the Schur-complement/Kron-reduction viewpoint; its composition rules
are established mathematics, not a proposed new hardness assumption.
[Kron reduction, Dörfler and Bullo](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.2950).

Boundary data can also be represented as voltage/current pairs (x,y), rather
than a response matrix. The graph y=Rx of a symmetric response matrix is a
Lagrangian subspace. This representation makes elementary boundary operations
linear and avoids singular matrix charts. Lam and Pylyavskyy connect electrical
operations with symplectic groups; Jekel treats boundary behavior over arbitrary
fields. We use these as mathematical starting points, not security results.
[Electrical networks and Lie theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.3475),
[Layering boundary graphs and networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.00247).

The retained revision replaces linear subspaces and linear operations with
finite samples of a nonlinear gradient graph and polynomial symplectic
permutations. Nonlinear boundary relations are already studied in network
mathematics; Johnson's work is relevant background. We do not import a real
network's positivity, monotonicity, or recoverability theorem into a finite
field. The prototype is an algebraic model inspired by boundary data, not a
claim that every compiled map is a passive electrical circuit.
[Nonlinear and signed conductors, Johnson](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.4045).

Here is the exact candidate family. Fix an odd prime p >= 5, an integer n >= 2,
a word length L >= 1, and a sample count 2 <= M <= p^n. Arithmetic is in F_p.
The ambient space has coordinates z=(x_1,...,x_n,y_1,...,y_n). These are research
parameters; no function of a security parameter or security level is endorsed.

For each coordinate i define two one-parameter polynomial permutations:

  E_i^+(t): x_i <- x_i + t y_i^3, all other coordinates unchanged;
  E_i^-(t): y_i <- y_i + t x_i^3, all other coordinates unchanged.

Their inverses use -t. They preserve the standard symplectic form, as one can
check directly by substituting into sum_i dx_i wedge dy_i. Assign indices
i < floor(n/2) to Alice, and all remaining indices to Bob, using zero-based
indices in the implementation. Every Alice elementary map commutes with every
Bob elementary map because their coordinate pairs are disjoint. Within one
party the upper and lower maps need not commute.

The coordinate decomposition is not supplied in its factorized form. During
parameter generation sample a polynomial automorphism

  phi = P o C_F o Q,             C_F(x,y) = (x + grad F(y), y).

F is a uniformly sampled nonzero homogeneous cubic in n variables: choose all
degree-three coefficients uniformly and resample the entire polynomial only
if every coefficient is zero. P and Q are independently sampled products of
2n linear symplectic shears. For each layer choose its upper/lower orientation
uniformly, v uniformly in F_p^n, and t uniformly in F_p, allowing zero values.
The upper layer is (x,y) -> (x+t v(v^T y),y); the lower layer adds t v(v^T x)
to y. No claim is made that this sampler is uniform on a symplectic group.

Publish each G_i^+(t,z) = phi E_i^+(t) phi^{-1}(z) and G_i^-(t,z), expanded
as ordinary polynomial coefficient lists in (z,t). Publish which generator
indices belong to each party, but not P,Q,F or their composition circuit.
The prototype compiles and verifies these maps using its local setup witness,
then returns only the coefficient lists. The sender and decapsulation code
use those lists. Python does not provide secure memory erasure; this is a
mathematical separation of inputs, not a memory-handling implementation claim.

Both phi and phi^{-1} have degree at most two. Therefore every published
generator has coordinate degree at most 2*3*2=12 and parameter t degree at
most two. All generators remain bijections and cross-party commutation is
preserved by conjugation. These statements follow algebraically and do not
depend on the empirical checks. Hiding a factorization is not a proof that it
cannot be recovered; nonlinearity alone is not a security argument.

Choose the public base set S0 by sampling M distinct x values uniformly without
replacement from F_p^n and attaching y=grad F0(x), where

  F0(x) = (1/2) sum_i x_i^2 + sum_{i=1}^{n-1} x_i^2 x_{i+1}^2.

Thus y_i = x_i + 2 x_i sum_{j adjacent to i in the path} x_j^2. The prototype
samples x values by rejecting duplicates and records the number of draws.
These duplicate rejections are part of the specified sampling procedure,
not discarded agreement failures. The base set is public and sorted.

Let theta consist of p,n,L,M,S0 and all expanded generator maps. The underlying
family is fully specified by this sampler. In a KEM interpretation, generate
theta during KeyGen and include it in the public key. The experiments make one
setup and one public key per profile. Reusing theta across receivers would be
a different deployment choice, not something established by these results.

A private word is sampled as L independent choices. Each choice selects a
generator index uniformly from the party's family and t uniformly from F_p.
Apply the selected generators in sample order. Include t=0, repeated indices,
and every resulting word. Do not condition on nontriviality, apparent mixing,
or an estimated orbit size. Write a and b for the resulting permutations.

The honest algorithms are:

  KeyGen:
    Generate theta as above. Sample Alice's word a. Compute SA=a(S0).
    Return pk=(theta,Encode(SA)) and sk=(a,pk).

  Encaps(pk):
    Read theta and SA. Sample Bob's word b independently.
    Set ct=Encode(b(S0)); set Z=Encode(b(SA)).
    Return (ct,KDF(theta,Encode(SA),ct,Z)).

  Decaps(sk,ct):
    Decode ct as a canonical set SB of M distinct ambient points.
    On an invalid encoding return failure.
    Otherwise set Z'=Encode(a(SB)) and return the same KDF with Z'.

Encaps and Decaps evaluate public generators pointwise using their own private
word. They never need the parameter-generation witness or the other party's
word. Secret words remain compact even if their full polynomial expansions
become large. The sample set is not the entire boundary variety; its use in
place of a full relation changes the proposed security problem.

Encode sorts the M distinct tuples lexicographically, keeps their coordinate
order (x then y), and writes each canonical field element as a fixed-width
big-endian integer of w=ceil(bit_length(p)/8) bytes. The point part of the public
key and the ciphertext each have exactly 2nMw bytes. Total public-key size also
includes theta, often overwhelmingly larger than the point part.

The parameter encoding is ASCII JSON with sorted dictionary keys and no extra
whitespace. Each of the 2n generators has 2n coordinate polynomials; each
polynomial is a sorted list of [exponent_vector,nonzero_coefficient] pairs.
The exponent vector has length 2n+1, with t last. The parameter identifier is
SHA-256 of this canonical JSON. The illustrative KDF is SHAKE256 returning
32 bytes on the concatenation

  b"crucible-nonlinear-candidate-v1\x00" || identifier ||
  Encode(SA) || ct || Z.

Within a parameter profile all three point-set strings have fixed lengths.
The domain separator is an ASCII string followed by one zero byte. No claim
of a security reduction to SHAKE256 is made. The public-parameter reloader in
the script reads generated evidence; it is not a validator for arbitrary
untrusted polynomial parameter sets.

Correctness lemma (proved). For every honestly generated theta and every
pair of sampled words, Decaps returns Encaps' key with zero mathematical
agreement failures. Each generator is a conjugate of a polynomial bijection,
so it preserves the number of distinct points. Cross-party commutation of
generators extends to their words. Consequently

  a(SB) = a(b(S0)) = b(a(S0)) = b(SA).

Sorting identical point sets produces identical encodings and therefore the
same KDF input. There are no divisions in the secret operations, no singular
response-matrix charts, and no reconciliation step. Encoding checks reject
wrong lengths, out-of-range field elements, duplicate points, and noncanonical
ordering. Arbitrary correctly encoded sets are accepted by this toy Decaps;
membership in the honestly generated orbit is not checked. None of these
correctness statements establishes IND-CCA security.

For fixed generator degree the public coefficient representation is finite and
polynomial in n, but the exponent of that polynomial is large. A loose support
bound per output coordinate is 3*binomial(2n+12,12), allowing t degrees 0,1,2.
There are 4n^2 output coordinates across the public maps. Evaluation costs are
polynomial in the published representation length, L, M, and log p. This
formal efficiency is a weak statement: actual coefficient expansion is already
expensive in very small examples.

Measured evidence (Python 3.14.6, seeded random.Random, not a cryptographic RNG):

  n   p    L   M   seed       agreement   coefficients   theta bytes
  2   7    4   8   20261015     16/16          3,368          54,185
  2   101  8  16   20261016     16/16         31,350         531,713
  3   7    4  16   20261017      8/8         406,232       8,128,897

The point public-key/ciphertext sizes were respectively 32, 64, and 96 bytes;
including parameters, public keys were 54,217, 531,777, and 8,128,993 bytes.
All keys were 32 bytes. All 40 encapsulations agreed across these three public
keys. There were also 140 generator expansion/inverse/identity checks and 12
encoding rejection checks. The public parameters were serialized and reloaded
before every profile's key generation and agreement experiments, so the
factored setup witness was unavailable to those operations.

Observed setup times were about 0.050, 0.592, and 22.440 seconds. Median combined
Encaps/Decaps times were about 0.0134, 0.415, and 1.621 seconds. These are
unoptimized, concurrent-run measurements on one machine, not controlled
benchmarks. The samples use different conjugators and fields; they do not
establish a universal scaling law. No honest failures were dropped. The small
field sizes and word lengths are demonstration parameters, not security tiers.

  python3 scripts/kem_discovery_nonlinear.py --seed 20261017 --n 3 --length 4 --points 16 --trials 8 --summary-only --output /tmp/nonlinear-n3.json

The checked-in files are nonlinear-results.json, nonlinear-101-results.json,
and nonlinear-n3-results.json. The first includes the expanded public maps and

do not use python -O. No application dependencies or database are involved.

The proposed computational problem is precise but remains a hypothesis to
study: sample theta,a,b exactly as above, reveal theta,SA=a(S0),SB=b(S0), and
ask for Encode(ab(S0)). This is the common-image problem for this sampled
action. Recovering a private word or the hidden conjugator would be sufficient
in some settings, but neither recovery problem is the definition of the target.
No worst-case-to-average-case reduction or distributional hardness evidence is
claimed. Increasing n,L,p,M does not currently come with a security estimate.

The intended initial security target would be IND-CPA: give an observer an
honestly generated pk, an honest ct, and either its derived key or an independent
uniform 32-byte string. The observer gets no decapsulation oracle. Difficulty
of computing the entire common image is not by itself a proved statement that
these two key distributions are indistinguishable. A suitable distributional
assumption and a KDF/model argument would still be required. IND-CCA, malicious
parameter handling, repeated-instance security, and quantum resistance are
unassessed. No transformation to IND-CCA is asserted or implemented. Per the
revised scope, no concerted cryptanalytic experiments were run on this revision.

The literature comparison puts clear limits on a novelty claim:

* The commuting-action agreement identity is known. Maze, Monico, and Rosenthal
  formulate semigroup-action key exchange, including a common-image problem.
  Our two individually noncommutative families commute with each other; their
  elementary maps act on finite point sets. This is a concrete instance of
  that broader design pattern, not a new agreement principle. It does not
  establish a reduction to ordinary finite-field discrete logarithms.
  [Semigroup actions](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0501017).

* Hidden composition of tame polynomial transformations is also an old
  cryptographic idea. Moh's TTM uses a secret factorization and a public
  polynomial map. Our published objects are conjugated one-parameter maps,
  and our secret per-message object is a word acting on a public point sample.
  Those are real differences in inputs and mechanism, but the underlying
  family resemblance is substantial. No distribution-preserving reduction in
  either direction was proved. The source's historical security assertions
  are not adopted here.
  [Moh's original exposition](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee380/9899fall/lect04X.html).

* Ustimenko and Wroblewska study key exchange with polynomial maps whose
  iterates have bounded degree. Their construction exchanges polynomial
  iterates. Here only generators have bounded degree; words are evaluated
  pointwise and their image samples are transmitted. We have not supplied a
  translation between full maps and these samples, or between their exponent
  distribution and our random-word distribution. Nonlinear polynomial maps
  in a key exchange are therefore not, on their own, a novelty claim.
  [Polynomial maps of stable degree](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.2920).

* Suzuki and Nuida improve a polynomial-map protocol that reconstructs a key
  through constrained polynomial preimages. Our honest algorithm applies
  permutations and uses commutation; it does not solve those preimage
  equations. Merely writing our finite computations as polynomial equations
  would not preserve their instance distribution or establish substantive
  equivalence. Their correctness and security estimates do not transfer here.
  [Polynomial-map protocol](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05924).

The initial linear baseline has a relationship to the established linear
decomposition literature. That connection was identified before the scope
change and is retained only as provenance, without extending that investigation.
[Myasnikov and Roman'kov](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.6401).

Sources were located using the actual mathematical objects: network response
matrices, finite-field boundary behavior, electrical symplectic actions,
nonlinear boundary relations, tame polynomial transformations, and polynomial
key exchange. This was a targeted comparison, not an exhaustive review. No
independence from established assumptions is proved. The strongest justified
classification is an inspectable variant in polynomial-action research, with
an explicit sampler and a correct agreement mechanism. Its status remains
retained and unvalidated, rather than a new established hardness assumption.

The next useful construction experiment is to reduce coefficient growth while
keeping the setup factorization absent from public parameters. Compare a sparse
cubic potential and a short stack of sparse nonlinear shears with the present
dense cubic, recording exact coefficient support, generator degree, setup size,
and honest agreement. Change one sampler feature at a time and preserve the
existing profiles. This would resolve whether a practically sized public
representation exists beyond these tiny examples. Smaller representations or
greater nonlinear depth must not be reported as improved security without
separate justification; that work is outside the current requested scope.
