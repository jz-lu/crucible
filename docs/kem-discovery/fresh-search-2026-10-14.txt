Fresh KEM search: local consistency and compact state representations
2026-10-14

Scope

This pass began from local update rules whose consistency identities allow
different update orders to agree. It did not start by modifying the retained
boundary-action construction. The work consisted of primary-source reading,
algebraic derivation, construction, and exact size arithmetic. No attacks,
candidate code, stress tests, trial parameter instances, or submissions were
performed. Not every mathematical direction below is a KEM candidate.

Main result

compact-transfer-candidate.txt records CTF1: a fully specified public local
permutation circuit on binary vectors, a known reversible Yang-Baxter local
map, commuting transfer maps, exact sampling, an agreement/KEM-wrapper draft,
and pen-and-paper correctness and cost arguments.

Its complete encoded public key has

    4 + uleb128_bytes(m) + ceil((44m^2+12m)/8) bytes,

including all public rule parameters and both global states. Its ciphertext
has ceil((16m^2+4m)/8) bytes. Honest word evaluation has O(m^4) elementary bit
cost and O(m^2) space. A consequence of the transfer-product identity reduces
the worst-case local-call count from 16m^2 to 8m^2 without changing the sampled
transformation. These formulas are not measured performance.

The preliminary direct-quadratic rule is preserved in
compact-transfer-initial-variant.txt. Moving to a permutation rule was a
constructive choice with a specific algebraic guarantee, not a security result.

CTF1 clears the representation-size part of the brief in the structural
parameter m. It does NOT yet clear the relationship-to-security requirement.
No lower bound or well-supported conjectural scaling constant relates m to
classical or quantum security bits. Thus there are no 128/192/256-bit parameter
recommendations and no justification for implementation or stress testing yet.

How the mathematical route developed

Transfer maps offered an initial mechanism: a small two-site rule induces
commuting transformations on a long state. This separates the size of a public
description from the degree of its fully composed polynomial expansion. The
rule can be evaluated directly, while the state length remains fixed.

Veselov's transfer theorem is the relevant identity:
  A. P. Veselov, Yang-Baxter maps and integrable dynamics, Theorem 1.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0205335

Some familiar examples are birational; a formula that works at generic complex
points does not automatically give a total map on all sampled finite-field
states. No such rational example was turned into a candidate here. Instead the
search followed finite algebraic constructions with total maps.

Braces supply many such constructions. The discussion of radical rings and
classical braces in Smoktunowicz/Vendramin was useful background; no new
radical-ring KEM assumption was claimed. Simple/asymmetric-product braces
then led to the more flexible function-parametrized construction used in CTF1.
  A. Smoktunowicz and L. Vendramin, On skew braces.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.06958
  D. Bachiller, F. Cedo, E. Jespers and J. Okninski, Asymmetric product of
  left braces and simplicity; new solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08493

Cedo/Okninski Section 3 gives the needed family for any j satisfying
j(x)=j(-x). Characteristic two makes that condition automatic. This permits
the explicit compact permutation circuit while retaining an exact local
consistency identity. The main note includes a direct binary braid calculation.
  F. Cedo and J. Okninski, New simple solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation
  and solutions associated to simple left braces.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07271

Other mathematical directions retained as observations

1. Tetrahedron maps. These give higher-dimensional consistency identities.
   For example, the published associative-ring map
   (X,Y,Z) -> (X,Y+XMZ,Z) has compact matrix input/output and an explicit
   inverse obtained by subtraction. The existence of this local identity
   does not itself specify a two-party common-secret protocol. In particular,
   a theorem about commuting transfer MATRICES, which can act on an enormous
   vector space, is not automatically a theorem about compact deterministic
   state maps. No suitable global boundary prescription and KEM mechanism
   were derived in this pass, so this observation was not promoted.
   S. Igonin, Set-theoretical solutions to the Zamolodchikov tetrahedron
   equation on associative rings and Liouville integrability, Theorem 1.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05552
   S. Igonin and S. Konstantinou-Rizos, Algebraic and differential-geometric
   constructions of set-theoretical solutions to the Zamolodchikov
   tetrahedron equation.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05998

2. Abelian processor networks. Order-independent stabilization is another
   possible source of agreement. The literature already identifies a critical
   group and a quotient of a lattice associated with the network. Those
   structural connections must be carried into any novelty comparison;
   a new name for stabilization would not establish an independent assumption.
   No secret-assisted computational gap, security distribution, or appropriate
   honest-runtime bound was supplied here. This remains a mathematical note.
   B. Bond and L. Levine, Abelian networks III. The critical group.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0170

3. Finite-field cellular automata. A recent construction studies a different
   function-parametrized Yang-Baxter family and reports finite-field examples.
   This is background, not evidence about CTF1. A table describing an arbitrary
   function on 2^d inputs is too large in d; any use of that route needs a
   compact sampled family with its own accounting. No table enumeration was
   performed and no experimental claims were imported into this research.
   A. Araoka and T. Tokihiro, Integrable cellular automata on finite fields
   of order 2^n.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17148

Novelty comparison actually completed

The generic protocol is a common-image semigroup/group-action construction,
not a new agreement pattern. The precise abstract translation for CTF1 is:
state set (A^2)^N, abelian transformation group generated by the T_i, and
private elements drawn by independent binary selectors, not uniformly over
that group. This does not by itself identify a hard known distribution.
  G. Maze, C. Monico and J. Rosenthal, Public Key Cryptography based on
  Semigroup Actions.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0501017

Chouraqui's paper establishes that cryptographic use of Yang-Baxter solutions
is not new as a category. Its tentative key exchange in Section 5 requires
participants to share knowledge of an underlying solution that is hidden from
others. CTF1 instead publishes its entire rule and keeps only independent
transfer selectors private. This is a public-data/mechanism distinction;
no distribution-preserving equivalence or separation is established.
  F. Chouraqui, Yang-Baxter equation and cryptography.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.13807

Focused searches included "Yang-Baxter maps integrable dynamics transfer",
"skew braces", "simple left braces asymmetric product", "Yang-Baxter key
exchange", "Yang-Baxter Feistel", and "transfer maps cryptography". They
located the primary constructions above but did not establish the novelty of
the particular CTF1 sampler. No conclusion is based on search-result absence.
No attack implementations or attack experiments were used.

Useful next question

Can the exact common-image distribution in CTF1 be connected to an independently
motivated average-case problem, or otherwise support a credible conjecture that
security grows linearly in a stated structural parameter? Local invertibility,
indecomposability, irretractability, large state spaces and selector lengths do
not answer that question. Until it has a substantive answer, keep CTF1 as a
research lead and continue mathematical discovery without stress testing it.
