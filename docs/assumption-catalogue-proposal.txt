CRUCIBLE — ASSUMPTION CATALOGUE AND ADMISSION PROPOSAL
2026-10-14 | Design candidate, not an activated registry or scoring policy

Purpose

Expand the useful attack surface without turning the site into an encyclopedia.
A visible entry should offer a distinct investigation, not merely a new label.
The precise scientific registry can be richer than the default browsing view.

User requirements recorded

- Attention is scarce: poorly organized assumptions hurt participation.
- Coarse security comparisons do not justify a combinatorial catalogue of variants.
- Distinguish assumptions discovered through Crucible from previously identified
  assumptions. This means origin, not recency or first import into the database.
- Retain the current KEM numerical challenge scope and manual review before activation.
- Preserve exact definitions, attribution, correctness, and automation interfaces.

Recommendation: shallow browsing, exact evidence underneath

Use one visible canonical entry for each curated research target or problem
cluster. Organize those entries under shallow family headings. Expand exact
variants and related KEM constructions inside the entry; avoid another sequence
of nested folder pages. A scheme-focused view may reuse the same metadata.
No empty family headings on the live site. No automatic Cartesian product of
ring, secret distribution, noise, sample count, attack model, or parameter size.

Do not equate browsing groups with mathematical equivalence or independence.
Plain LWE and module-LWE can share an LWE entry while retaining distinct formal
specifications and distinct KEM constructions. RSA inversion and factoring are
related targets, not synonyms. CDH, DLP and DDH remain different games.
A shared folder never transfers a recovery, a red graph marker, or a score.

A visible variant must answer: What useful experiment or participant decision
would we lose if this variant were omitted? Examples include a meaningful change
in algebraic structure, a materially different input distribution or attack
interface, or a documented scientific comparison. An extra name, encoding, or
routine parameter choice alone does not earn another card or prize lineage.
Important distinctions still belong in the exact specification even when they
are hidden from the default view.

Origin categories

Proposed visible filters: All | Crucible discoveries | Previously identified.
These filter the same canonical records; they do not create duplicate registries.

- Crucible discovery: reviewed evidence that this specified assumption originated
  through Crucible. Retain credited contributors, dated submission/revision,
  precise claimed novelty, closest prior work, and the review decision.
- Previously identified: identified before the Crucible contribution, whether in
  a publication, preprint, unpublished manuscript, or another documented source.
- Not established: provenance remains unresolved. Do not infer discovery merely
  because a search or the catalogue contains no matching name.

Treat discovery as a revisable, attributed provenance claim. Review cannot prove
that every earlier idea has been found. A later correction changes the provenance
record through an audit trail, not the original challenge receipts.

Store assumption origin and construction origin separately. A new KEM using a
known assumption can be a Crucible construction without being a new assumption.
A novel exact variant does not automatically establish a new mathematical family.
Importing a supplied paper is not itself evidence of a platform discovery;
authorship by Crucible contributors alone does not establish platform origin.

Keep origin separate from:
- scientific maturity / literature and standardization status;
- review state and catalogue publication;
- executable challenge availability;
- security assessment and exact accepted attack results;
- contribution awards and scoring enrollment.
Known does not mean secure; discovered here does not mean reviewed or runnable.
No automatic 1,000-point award for importing an assumption, changing a label,
creating a variant, or being placed in the discovery filter. Existing reviewed
new-KEM admission awards retain their current separate policy.

A deliberately small expansion

Retain the two current live KEM controls and prepare three reference candidates:

1. Finite-field Diffie–Hellman: retain the actual hashed shared-secret target,
   group distribution, reuse policy and pinned release. Do not relabel it as a
   generic test of every DLP/CDH/DDH formulation.
2. RSA inversion: retain the current RSA-based KEM. Factorization is an attack
   route; unrestricted equivalence is not asserted.
3. ML-KEM: module-LWE anchor; pin the exact standard, implementation and official
   parameter set. Do not invent fifty standardized ML-KEM parameter sets.
4. FrodoKEM: an intentional contrast with plain/unstructured LWE. Start with one
   selected variant. AES/SHAKE choices and ordinary parameter levels are not
   separate mathematical families or front-page cards.
5. HQC: a code-based KEM contrast; pin the actual quasi-cyclic distributions,
   construction, decoder, specification revision and decoding-failure analysis.

This is five scheme pages across four broad browsing families, not five claims
of independent hardness. Two are live today; the three references are not yet
admitted challenges. Reference-only entries belong in a clearly marked catalogue
view rather than masquerading as available attacks.

Implementation order should be one lattice representative and HQC, then the second
lattice representative when the structured/unstructured comparison is explicitly
supported. The ordering between ML-KEM and FrodoKEM depends on whether the next
step prioritizes a standardized anchor or an easily inspected tunable benchmark.
Do not decide it by the number of variants an implementation library exposes.

Keep low-noise LPN and symplectic-LPN/LSN constructions as a focused research
comparison in the private admission backlog. Avoid an indiscriminate menu of all
noise regimes. Keep NTRU/LWR, more code distributions, isogenies, multivariate
constructions and signature-only targets deferred until a concrete proposal earns
its place. This is a launch scope decision, not a claim that they are unimportant.

The tier constraint

A standard with three named parameter sets cannot honestly become fifty standard
variants just by relabelling them. Small solvable derivatives require separate,
explicit experimental construction/release identities and their own correctness
and attack-cost analysis. Evidence from them does not mark the standard broken.
Do not force every future scheme to have a dense 1–50 ladder if the sampler,
correctness conditions or meaningful parameters do not support one.

Tier comparisons remain approximate reference choices. Preserve model, estimator
version, calibration date and actual parameters; do not infer an ordering between
assumptions from tier labels. Compare measured bytes directly under named encoding
conventions. Timing needs a common benchmark. Reported attack compute is evidence
with declared limitations, not a directly enforced common budget.

Paper-specific review findings

Classification brief, pp.3–8 and 13–16:
- Adopt the distinction between family, formal distribution/game and scheme.
- Adopt directed, parameter-qualified relationships and separate review states.
- Decline its p.16 broad launch list and p.15 three simultaneous attack tracks for
  this MVP. Preserve those as possible future extensions.

Symplectic LPN between two noise regimes of LPN, pp.1–2:
- The draft relates different noise regimes. Its stated reductions do not give
  matched-parameter equivalence or directly comparable concrete attack costs.
- Represent exact endpoints, direction, parameter map, success loss, runtime
  overhead and model. These are supplied draft claims, not independently checked
  proofs or automatic evidence propagation rules.

Post-Quantum Cryptography from Quantum Stabilizer Decoding, Construction 5.1 p.23:
- The displayed construction encrypts one bit. A KEM adaptation still needs a
  full-secret distribution, ciphertext format, KDF, decoding/rejection behavior
  and exact security claim. Hashing one bit to a long string does not add entropy.
- Whole-secret correctness must cover the chosen finite parameters, all repetitions
  and exceptional keys. A base one-bit success probability is not that bound.
- Repetition changes the attacker-visible sample count; freeze it in the contract.
- Reconcile overlapping claims across draft versions during review rather than
  silently choosing a claim from whichever PDF was imported most recently.

Proposed pipeline

1. Extract a candidate from a paper or submission.
   Record source, exact page/definition, author-stated claim and unresolved fields.
   Automated extraction is a draft. Missing definitions are not guessed.
2. Match it against existing canonical records.
   Suggest aliases, existing variants and related constructions. Show the closest
   entries and a concise 'what changes / why it matters' explanation. Human review
   decides equivalence and placement; similarity alone must not merge definitions.
3. Review the formal specification and origin claim privately.
   Decide: reference to existing record, new formal variant, new construction,
   or a genuinely new assumption proposal. Pin immutable revisions and decisions.
4. Publish a reviewed catalogue entry if useful.
   Catalogue publication may precede a live challenge. Display availability
   explicitly; keep unresolved drafts out of the active challenge list.
5. Review and integrate an executable KEM challenge separately.
   Pin KeyGen/Encaps/Decaps, sampler and validation, public/secret encoding, setup
   and reuse, correctness scope/bound, allowed parameters, answer verifier,
   reference attack and initial estimates. Use trusted code integration; do not
   execute uploaded implementations in the web service.
6. Activate the reviewed exact release/profile.
   Catalogue origin does not activate a challenge or enroll a scoring lineage.
   Reviewer records the activation and any separate, authorized admission credit.

Minimum structured concepts

- BrowsingFamily: a navigation label, aliases and cross-links; not a score family.
- AssumptionDefinitionRevision: stable ID/version, exact problem and goal,
  distribution/conditioning, parameter family, secret and noise distributions,
  public auxiliary input, oracle access, adversary model, success condition,
  source definition and claimed hardness. Unknowns remain explicit.
- AssumptionRelationship: endpoints, typed direction, parameter transformation,
  cost/success loss, assumptions/model, citation, review and disputed status.
- SchemeRelease: existing immutable KEM contract plus version-pinned reviewed
  links to the assumptions used by a particular security claim.
- ChallengeProfile: existing executable parameter contract and exact release.
- OriginReview: object type/revision, origin claim, dated evidence, attribution,
  closest prior work, decision, reviewer and later corrections.
- CataloguePublication: curated parent, visibility and availability; independent
  of implementation registry and existing scored KEM lineage.

Existing-code implications

protocol_specs.Security.assumptions is currently free text. Add structured links
through a new specification version or separately reviewed link records; do not
change canonical hashes of approved v1 specifications.
protocol_proposals already supplies private intake, immutable revisions and
reviewer decisions. Reuse those mechanics; add a distinct assumption-only review
contract rather than forcing mathematical assumptions into a fake KEM schema.
registry.py's explicit trusted adapters remain the only execution path.
scoring_awards.family_id means an admitted KEM scoring lineage, not a taxonomy
family. Keep these separate to avoid creating rewards by adding categories.

Do not enable the dormant Research implementation as the assumptions pipeline.
Its hardcoded DH/DLog mappings and public unreviewed contribution endpoints do
not provide the version-pinned private admission workflow required here. A hidden
navigation tab is not an access-control mechanism. Review that subsystem before
reuse; the current proposal here does not activate it.
