CORRECTNESS CLAIMS AND ASSUMPTIONS — 17 OCTOBER 2026

Admission requires a precise correctness guarantee and a scoped security claim.
Both may depend on explicitly stated, unproved assumptions. Inclusion makes a
scheme available for research; it does not certify the truth of those claims.
New approval and admission require a claimed honest-failure probability at most
2^-128 for every admitted configuration, or perfect agreement. This is a required
claimed goal under explicit premises, not a requirement to prove every premise.
The policy identifier remains crucible-honest-agreement-128-v1.

Correctness claim

State either perfect honest agreement (failure bound "0") or a precise statistical
failure bound at most 2^-128, with probability scope, parameter coverage,
assumptions and the reasoning connecting them to the bound. Bounds may be conditional,
model-based or conjectural. Identify exactly which steps are proved, modeled or
unproved. A scheme need not prove every premise before it can be studied.
Unknown correctness and weaker claimed bounds are allowed in intake drafts.
Before new proposal approval or live-challenge admission, the proposal must claim
the required target and provide its dependencies and supporting reasoning. Merely
changing the numerical label without a justified argument is insufficient.

Security claim

State the security notion, adversary/oracle model, claimed guarantee and parameter
scope, along with its assumptions. Distinguish asymptotic claims from finite
attack estimates. No invented numerical bit estimate is required for a novel
claim, and deliberately small research stages may have different stated strength.
Reviewers check that the claim and dependencies are explicit and worth studying;
approval does not establish hardness, correctness or IND-CCA security. Where
a security argument depends on a correctness bound, record the required threshold
and dependency explicitly; claims must not silently borrow stronger assurances.

Evidence and assumptions

correctness.justification.kind distinguishes:
  mathematical-proof — a mathematical proof under its listed premises;
  analytical-bound — a derived bound with explicit conditions;
  model-based-analysis — a calculation in an explicitly idealized model;
  conjectural-claim — a precise claimed guarantee with motivated unproved steps.

Each uses statement, conditions, configuration_coverage and derivation. For a
conjectural claim, derivation records rationale and the unproved dependencies,
not an invented proof. Independently motivate assumptions rather than merely
restate the desired conclusion: assuming that the KEM already meets the target
is tautological and does not supply a meaningful justification. Premises must
specify mathematical or algorithmic properties that can be assessed or challenged
independently of the target conclusion. Examine dependencies, rare events, weak keys,
parameter changes and whether weaker assumptions would suffice. Identify what
could support or refute the conjectural steps. A family argument can cover many
configurations without redundant per-stage papers.

A checked computation under model M does not establish that M describes the
algorithm. If the modeled failure is <=delta and its probability differs from
reality by at most epsilon, the real bound is only delta+epsilon. Ordinary
empirical agreement cannot establish negligible epsilon. Finite tests may expose
mistakes but cannot establish an extreme tail or prove an assumption. Preserve
conditionality in downstream security arguments, graph summaries and reviews.

Refutation and challenges to the justification

A rigorous demonstration that the actual honest-failure probability exceeds
2^-128 in the claimed configuration and experiment refutes the target claim for
that scope. This need not refute a conditional theorem whose premises fail.
Distinguish an actual lower bound from a loose upper bound: proving
only delta<=2^-64 leaves the required target unestablished by that argument; it
does not prove delta>2^-128. Disproving a narrower claimed bound may require its
own comparison even when the common target still holds.

A counterexample to an assumption, an invalid inference, a missing proof or a
model mismatch undermines the justification. It does not alone establish that
the actual failure probability exceeds the target; the scheme may meet the target
for another reason. A single failing instance warrants investigation but does
not by itself establish the failure probability under the declared honest
sampling distribution. Statistical claims permit rare failures; distinguish
properly scoped statistical inference from a rigorous probability bound. A single
disagreement found adversarially can refute perfect agreement if it lies in the
claimed honest-input support and satisfies the premises. It does not alone
refute a statistical 2^-128 claim: probability under the specified distribution
matters. Record separately which claim, assumption or reasoning step is affected.

These distinctions guide manual review and research notes. They do not add an
automatic refutation rule, visualizer or new attack-submission pipeline.

Claim dependencies should remain understandable to humans and agents. The research
notebook can hold precise assumptions, supporting arguments and counterexamples;
curated scheme views can later explain their consequences. This policy does not
claim a complete dependency visualizer has already been implemented.

Experiment and execution

For each configuration, sample an honest key pair, encapsulate honestly and
decapsulate with its matching private key. Different output or honest rejection
is a correctness failure. State the probability space, distributions, setup,
conditioning and parameter scope; an average-over-keys claim is not a per-key
or adversarial-ciphertext guarantee.

Report generation/resource aborts separately, including rates and retry costs.
A disagreement cannot be silently resampled into success. If alpha bounds
excluded aborts and delta is conditional disagreement, total exchange failure
is bounded by alpha+(1-alpha)*delta. This does not justify an unclaimed availability
guarantee. The live service retains unexpected disagreements for review.

Implementation conformance and safe execution remain separate requirements:
precise pseudocode, encodings, reviewed pinned source/artifacts, malformed-input
handling, bounded resources and reproducible checks. Submitted code stays inert
until trusted integration. Relaxing evidentiary certainty does not relax these
execution controls, access permissions or private-data protections.

Required target and prospective metadata

2^-128 is Crucible's required claimed correctness goal for new approvals and
admissions. It is not a security-bit claim or a NIST-mandated numerical cutoff.
Preserve stronger per-configuration bounds within the reviewed common envelope.
A security reduction or usage requirement may demand a stricter bound.

correctness.policy_id = "crucible-honest-agreement-128-v1" identifies this policy
but is optional. For a statistical claim, new approval/admission requires
correctness.failure_bound_exponent as a conservative integer b>=128 declaring
delta<=2^-b under the stated premises. Review consistency with failure_bound,
justification and parameter coverage; the server does not evaluate formulas or
prove the assertion. Round fractional exponents down and retain the tighter
expression in the reasoning. Perfect claims state failure_bound "0" and omit
the exponent. Unknown claims also omit it and remain draft-only. Weak statistical
claims and missing exponents remain valid drafts but cannot receive new approval
or admission. Historical omitted fields retain their canonical hashes; prior
records are not retroactively certified or rewritten.
