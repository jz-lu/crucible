PUBLICATION SCREEN V3 — SCOPE AND EVALUATION
2026-11-06 | candidate wording; no provider result claimed here

DECISION

The publication screen should also send obviously empty/off-topic posts and
needlessly degrading or profanity-heavy attacks to human review. A flag is neither
a rejection nor a claim about the author's intentions. Scientific quality,
correctness, novelty, usefulness, prestige and mathematical maturity are not gates.

The new gratuitous_hostility category makes this distinction explicit: a tirade
against a result may need a language edit even if it is not targeted harassment
or serious harm. The existing targeted_abuse definition is unchanged. Spam covers
obvious filler or unrelated publications; it cannot stand in for "bad research".

Protected cases include direct criticism of Crucible or an institution, terse
questions, weak or false mathematical claims, speculative mathematics without a
known application, literature pointers and collaboration requests. An incidental
expletive is not a word-list veto. A relevant hostile quotation can be evidence,
not endorsement. A substantive technical point alongside gratuitous insults does
not make those insults acceptable; the smallest correction preserves the point.

The canonical wording and structured output contract are in
crucible/publication_screening_policy.py. V3 adds one output enum value, not a
new database table, score, automatic sanction, scientific review or provider call.
The publication notice and reviewer labels describe the expanded scope.

IDENTITY AND OLD WORK

The version, prompt hash and output-schema hash all change the policy identity.
Existing jobs retain their exact v2 policy snapshots. V3 workers do not send or
publish them as v3: queued mismatches become human-review work. Already published
revisions retain their original receipts and are not rescanned. In-flight results
must still satisfy the original lease and the current durable control checks.

A stale browser policy ID requires the author to read the new notice. A deployment
alone does not change the durable control to v3. The legacy-request adoption
helper excludes every request with any existing job and will reject a saved v2
plan. After validation and activation, the operator must create a new preview of
only the still-eligible original requests. Do not recreate the old 20 trial notes
or change the user's earlier test note. No migration or retrospective reclassification
is part of this change.

EVIDENCE AND EVALUATION

publication-screening-v3-cases.json contains 18 new development examples, including
contrastive pairs. They are proposed labels, not evidence the model agrees. Two
independent reviewers are preparing held-out sets without seeing this wording;
keep their contents out of prompt development until the candidate is frozen.
Existing v1/v2 evaluations remain historical evidence with their original hashes.

Offline tests cover serialization without evaluator-label leakage, valid new
category handling, private reviewer presentation and preservation of old-policy
jobs. They do not classify prose. The release coordinator will run the approved
model against frozen blind requests using the existing team key and pilot budget;
this draft does not read a key or call a provider.

For the model run, report legitimate contributions wrongly flagged separately
from empty/hostile material wrongly passed, with counts and case-level reasons.
Inspect whether cited passages support the finding and whether suggested changes
preserve the criticism. Coverage/transport/schema failures are operational human
fallbacks, never correct classifier judgments. Include the old benign/abuse cases
to check that broader relevance screening has not degraded cryptanalysis protections.
Do not lower thresholds or change labels merely to improve a score; an ambiguous
boundary needs a documented human decision. Any prompt revision needs a new digest
and fresh evidence, with development and first-use held-out results distinguished.

Small synthetic sets cannot establish a production error rate. After activation,
monitor manual reversals and genuine participant feedback before expanding scope.
The broader policy may create more false flags; retaining human review is deliberate.

Method reference: OpenAI Evaluation best practices recommends task-specific cases,
edge/adversarial coverage and human calibration instead of relying on impressions.
