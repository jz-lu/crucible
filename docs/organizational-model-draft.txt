CRUCIBLE: ORGANIZATIONAL MODEL FOR DISCUSSION
23 November 2026. Design draft, not an implementation or deployment claim.

Purpose and recommendation
--------------------------
Crucible should help people understand cryptographic ideas, investigate them,
and turn useful findings into carefully reviewed changes. The architecture
should distinguish those activities without making participants learn it first.
Use five functional compartments below. These are ownership boundaries, not
five required navigation tabs, five databases, or five separate services.
For a newcomer, the simpler mental model can be Understand / Investigate /
Contribute. Navigation should follow frequent tasks rather than the schema.

1. Research record and substantiation
------------------------------------
Owns: authored research, immutable revisions, precise statements, assumptions,
arguments, citations, uncertainty, and explicit relationships between findings.
Input: a question, conjecture, construction, proof, obstruction or observation.
Output: durable research that others can inspect, cite and challenge exactly.
The notebook is its primary authoring and reading surface. Experimental
receipts remain owned by the experiment system; notes cite and interpret them.

An assumption is a precise unproved premise used in an argument, not another
repository of prose. Give statements stable note-local IDs and exact revision
links. A dedicated assumption view can aggregate its uses and responses without
duplicating the definition. One note can contain several statements; several
arguments can use the same exact statement in different scopes.
The statement and its use must be distinct: record which conclusion depends
on it, the role it plays, parameter coverage and relevant model. A restriction
such as classical adversaries is not automatically an unproved assumption.
An authored 'supports' edge is not proof. Publication is not substantiation.

Pushback: turning every interesting sentence into a formal object would hinder
exploration. Ordinary notes should remain easy to write. Add precise statements
where reuse or a consequential dependency makes the extra structure valuable.
Separate assumptions into another compartment only if they acquire genuinely
independent operations, not merely because they deserve a prominent view.

2. Constructions and experiments
--------------------------------
Owns: exact scheme releases, parameter configurations, default progressions,
implementation identities, challenge protocols, measurements and result receipts.
Input: a defined construction, runnable implementation and explicit experiment.
Output: identified observations, with enough provenance to reproduce or dispute.
A stage is a position in one versioned progression; an instance is a particular
issued challenge. Neither is a universal measure of security. Historical result
identities must survive a changed progression or a better implementation.
The scheme record identifies what is running and cites its research sources;
it should not silently become a competing mathematical specification.

3. Work and incentives
---------------------
Owns: actionable tasks, completion criteria, verification plans, participation,
bounty allowance, boosts and earned-award records.
Input: a worthwhile question and an intelligible standard for completing it.
Output: coordinated work, contribution claims and recognition when justified.
A bounty is a task that cites research; it is not a kind of note. Its solution
can be explained in a contribution note. Exploration need not begin with a
bounty, and useful research need not earn points to deserve preservation.
An incentive rule allocates attention or recognition; it does not establish
mathematical validity or cross-scheme difficulty.

4. Decisions and adoption
------------------------
Owns: who may decide which transition, the exact evidence reviewed, the scope
and reasons of that decision, and the record of subsequent implementation.
Input: a deliberate request referring to exact research, task or release state.
Output: an accountable decision and, separately, evidence of follow-through.
Keep publication screening, substantive review and operational adoption distinct.
The first asks whether material is suitable to share. The second assesses a
specific proposed action or claim. The third establishes what actually changed.
Approved, in progress, completed and live must not be interchangeable labels.
Work coordination owns a task's promise; this compartment supplies the authority
for admitting the task or accepting a solution. The award ledger then records
the consequence. A boost cannot purchase approval, and a pending claim is not
an award. Human and agent interfaces must preserve these same boundaries.

5. Views and orientation
-----------------------
Owns: comprehensible projections, navigation, search, filters and reading context.
Input: authorized research, experiments, work and decisions.
Output: note lists, scheme pages, graphs, timelines, maps, boards and profiles.
A resource curve answers a different question from a premise-dependency graph,
a chronology, or a work-status board. Name that question and expose provenance.
Views should not maintain competing scientific status. Drawing an edge does
not establish a reduction; a recovery line does not establish a security bound.
Cached projections are appropriate, provided identity, freshness and permissions
remain correct. An exact citation never grants access to its target.

Contracts joining the compartments
---------------------------------
Research reference: note ID + revision + content hash; add a statement, argument
or section ID when a specific part is meant. Display numbers are conveniences.
Experiment reference: scheme/release + exact configuration + implementation and
protocol identity + receipt identity. Retain original inputs where applicable.
Task reference: immutable terms + exact source pins + exact contribution pins.
Decision reference: the submitted identity, authorized actor, outcome and scope.
Adoption reference: the decision plus evidence of the actual implementation or
release. A completed tracking event is not itself a deployment receipt.
Views join these identities. Labels, titles and stage numbers are not join keys.
These are conceptual contracts; they do not assert that every join has a live UI.

Stress tests
------------
New scheme: research definition and premises -> admission review -> implemented
release/configurations and tests -> catalogue. A bounty is optional.
Attack a premise: publish an exact objection and evidence -> expose affected
uses -> assess consequences. Do not automatically erase dependent findings.
Improve a decoder: state what changes -> compare correctness and costs under
identified settings -> review -> integrate. Preserve earlier measurements.
Solve a bounty: published contribution -> independent completion assessment ->
task settlement. Publishing a note alone does not complete its bounty.
Replicate a result: recover original inputs and identities -> produce a separate
receipt -> relate it to the original. Do not overwrite history or award a false
first breakthrough. Failed replication also needs a clear account of differences.

Capability boundary at this draft
---------------------------------
CURRENT SOURCE (09958b2): durable notes/revisions and exact citations; publication
screening with configured modes; human proposal/bounty decisions; versioned
challenges and receipts; CLI access; resource charts, maps, timeline and profiles.
Source capability alone does not prove a feature is enabled on the hosted site.
CURRENT SOURCE, LOCAL-ONLY WORKFLOW: precise statement/argument selection and
review experiments have local targets; do not portray them as hosted authority
over schemes, correctness, security curves or deployments.
LOCAL CHANGE IN THIS CHECKOUT: explicit implementation-progress events after
qualifying proposal decisions, including completion evidence and reopening.
These events do not automatically publish, deploy, admit schemes or award points.
PROPOSED HERE: this five-part model, a consistent end-to-end lineage view and
newcomer orientation. A comprehensive dependency-impact view remains design work.
No additional service, infrastructure or automatic scientific verifier is proposed.
