Lossless module-LWE research KEM — dimension-research-v2
Assessment date: 2026-10-15. Local research beta; not scientific admission.

Why this ladder replaces the preferred modulus ladder
-----------------------------------------------------
The previous modulus-research-v1 release held degree/noise mostly fixed and
reduced oversized moduli. Its later profiles were both stronger under a chosen
attack estimate and smaller in bytes. That was a deliberately weakened practice
ladder, not a useful illustration of the cost of increasing security. Its source,
profiles, estimates, vectors and historical submissions remain immutable.

This release instead increases the actual lattice dimension. Eleven distinct
natural profiles are registered, without invented intermediate tiers. The ring
degree is nondecreasing, and rank increases provide intermediate dimensions
before the degree rises again. Every degree is a power of two,
so x^n+1 remains the corresponding cyclotomic polynomial over the integers.
For each profile, q is the smallest prime satisfying the SAME perfect honest
correctness bound. Noise remains full CBD2; there is no ciphertext compression.

Larger profiles pay in coefficients, byte encodings and polynomial/matrix
arithmetic. This is still a selected research path, NOT a proof that these are
globally optimal parameter choices or that every larger profile has greater
actual confidentiality. The lossless format deliberately favors a simple exact
correctness argument over production-style compression. Wall-clock performance
depends on the implementation and hardware and must be benchmarked separately.

The initial endpoint target is approximately128 classical reference bits when
supported by analysis. The first natural endpoint here exceeds it: Tier11 has a
flattened-LWE heuristic reference of approximately137.547943 bits. This is a
discrete overshoot, not an exact128-bit or full-KEM security claim. We do not
weaken an otherwise sensible profile merely to hit a desired numerical target.
Eleven tiers do not imply comparability with ordinal tiers of another scheme.
Future harder profiles can extend the ladder; revised attack estimates belong
alongside the frozen initial reference, without silently redefining old tiers.
Intermediate ranks improve the ladder's coverage without reducing native
plaintext capacity at a later tier. Gaps in estimated attack cost remain large,
especially at higher dimensions; consecutive tiers are not a promise of small
or equal effort increments. More dense steps would need further justified
construction/parameter work rather than hidden entropy truncation or oversized
moduli chosen merely to weaken a level.

Native plaintexts and their explicit enumeration limitation
---------------------------------------------------------
One bit is carried by each ring coefficient, so the complete native plaintext
space is {0,1}^n: 8 bits at the smallest degree, growing to256 bits. Every native
plaintext is sampled with its full probability; we do not restrict the message
space of a nominal256-bit scheme or truncate the seed that samples s,e or A.
Key generation and private rejection each retain a full256-bit input seed.

The encapsulated key is the full32-byte KDF output at every profile. Its length
does not create entropy absent from the plaintext. With the public key and
ciphertext, an attacker can enumerate at most2^n native messages, derive coins,
and compare entire deterministic ciphertexts. This is an explicit attack in
candidate encryptions, not the same unit as an estimator's Core-SVP operations.
It is therefore reported separately rather than silently taking a numerical
minimum across incompatible cost units. The tiny profiles are openly weak.

scripts/lattice_solve_module_v2_tier1.py implements this attack using only a
public Tier1 challenge. A test forbids private generation, decapsulation and
fresh randomness during the solve, and verifies the returned full shared key
against a newly issued instance. This establishes a working public-input
example; it is not a general lattice attack or a higher-tier time guarantee.

Exact construction and framing
------------------------------
This is a separate, nonstandard KEM, inspired by primitives in FIPS203. It is
not standardized ML-KEM, not wire-compatible, and inherits neither ML-KEM's
security theorem nor its official known-answer vectors.
Reference: https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/FIPS/NIST.FIPS.203.pdf

R_q = Z_q[x]/(x^n+1), rank k, eta=2. Coefficients are serialized in increasing
degree using w=ceil(bit_length(q)/8) little-endian bytes, each strictly less
than q. All eleven current profiles have w=2. No lossy compression is applied.
Define BIND = uint16le(n)||uint8(k)||uint32le(q); all lengths are explicit.
Literal domains below are ASCII, without terminators. H=SHA3-256, G=SHA3-512.

Key generation draws d32||z32. Compute
  rho32||sigma32 = G("Crucible-Module-LWE-v2/keygen"||BIND||d32).
Matrix A[i,j] expands
  SHAKE128("Crucible-Module-LWE-v2/A"||BIND||rho32||uint8(i)||uint8(j)).
Read w-byte little-endian candidates, mask to bit_length(q), reject values>=q.
Expansion is capped at32*n candidates per polynomial; exceeding this cap aborts
without biased modulo substitution or resampling a favorable honest outcome.
Each polynomial is pseudorandom from a256-bit seed. Ideal independent XOF bits
induce uniform field coefficients through rejection; a finite seeded expansion
is not literally uniform over all possible full matrices.

Noise(seed32,nonce) expands n/2 bytes using
  SHAKE256("Crucible-Module-LWE-v2/noise"||BIND||seed32||uint8(nonce)).
Each four consecutive bits (b0,b1,b2,b3), in least-significant-bit order, yield
b0+b1-b2-b3. Thus the full CBD2 support is retained; ideal independent expansion
bits induce probabilities1,4,6,4,1 over -2,-1,0,1,2. No coefficient is truncated.
Use nonces0..k-1 for s, k..2k-1 for e, both under sigma. Set t=A*s+e.
  pk = "MLD2"||BIND||rho32||Encode(t).
  sk = z32||signed-int8(s)||pk||H(pk).
The public header and every derivation bind n,k,q; another profile's public key
cannot be silently reinterpreted under new parameters.

Encapsulation samples native mu of exactly n/8 bytes, uniformly over all n bits.
  K32||coins32 = G("Crucible-Module-LWE-v2/encap"||BIND||mu||H(pk)).
Use Noise(coins,0..k-1) for r, nonces k..2k-1 for e1, and2k for e2. Compute
  u=A^T*r+e1,
  v=t^T*r+e2+EncodeMessage(mu),
where bit i, taken least-significant-bit first, is multiplied by ceil(q/2).
The ciphertext is Encode(u)||Encode(v); return the full K32.

Decapsulation computes v-s^T*u, rounds each coefficient to the nearest message
center modulo q, derives coins from the recovered native mu and completely
reencrypts. It returns K32 only if every ciphertext byte agrees; otherwise it
returns SHAKE256(z32||ct,32). A length-correct noncanonical ciphertext also
returns this implicit-rejection key. Wrong sizes/types are format errors.
There is no public decapsulation oracle in Crucible.

Fixed correctness across every profile
-------------------------------------
The residual error is E=e^T*r-s^T*e1+e2. Every coefficient of a negacyclic
product of degree n contributes at most n*eta^2 in absolute value, hence
  |E_i| <= C(n,k)=2*k*n*eta^2+eta = 8*k*n+2.
Every profile chooses the smallest prime q with floor(q/4)>C(n,k). This suffices
for all n native message bits to decode for EVERY allowed noise draw. Faithful
arithmetic and full reencryption consequently give honest shared-key agreement.
The mathematical disagreement bound is the same delta=0 throughout the ladder;
independence assumptions are not needed for this deterministic support proof.

For Tier1, C=66 and floor(269/4)=67. For Tier11, C=6146 and floor(24593/4)=6148.
Small spare margin does not change the zero-disagreement claim: the proof
covers the entire declared support, including extremal errors. Resource aborts
and implementation faults are separate. The finite sampling cap and20-second
generator deadline can abort; the proof does not claim those operational
failure probabilities are zero. An issued challenge retains its original
encapsulated target and performs one honest agreement check, never resampling
until a passing example appears.

Calibration and evidence
------------------------
docs/lattice-module-v2-calibration.json contains actual outputs from the pinned
official lattice-estimator commit53da5982597709ba0fdf94ea37a84d822310fd84,
archiveSHA2564236adc38031c139c8a2721804d8962d6cacd015fae37f6f5215d2ac7f011060,
under Sage10.1. Exact prime proofs establish both primality and minimality above
the support threshold. The script verifies the archive and extracted files
before executing estimator code. No network access is needed for reproduction.

Each profile uses LWE.estimate.rough with flattened dimension n*k, m=n*k,
CBD2 secret/error, ADPS16 classical Core-SVP/GSA, primal uSVP and dual hybrid.
The first two dimensions are outside the tool's supported small-dimension
range; the next three hit its beta40 floor. They intentionally have no
numerical security estimate. Tiers6..11 yield approximately25.112,37.96,63.364,
88.185503,113.169697 and137.547943 reference bits respectively. No interpolation
is reported as a result.

This flattened approximation omits module/ring structure, additional ciphertext
information, full-KEM reductions, quantum attacks, side channels and multi-target
effects. Its numbers are heuristic attack-model costs, not measured work,
elapsed seconds or lower bounds on confidentiality. Perfect correctness does
not establish any confidentiality claim or deployment suitability.

Reproduce with Sage10.1:
  sage -python scripts/lattice_calibrate_module_v2.py \
    --estimator /path/to/lattice-estimator-53da5982597709ba0fdf94ea37a84d822310fd84 \
    --archive /path/to/pinned-estimator.tar.gz --output /tmp/module-v2-calibration.json
  sage -python scripts/lattice_module_v2_reference.py \
    --calibration /tmp/module-v2-calibration.json --output /tmp/module-v2-vectors.json

Independent Sage quotient-ring vectors cover all eleven parameter profiles,
including native plaintext lengths and independent nearest-center decoding.
They are research vectors, not official NIST vectors. Tests also cover every
issued profile, all256 Tier1 plaintexts, actual public-only recovery, exact
support endpoints and every scalar error in the proven support interval,
canonical encodings, implicit rejection, parameter/domain binding, sampler
aborts, source-change races and generation deadlines.

The generator is variable-time Python with bounded output and a20-second
process deadline. Its child reports the executed source digest; the parent
checks that digest and checks its registered source before and after the run.
Runtime/OS provenance is recorded only to the documented artifact scope, not
a complete dependency closure. Independent cryptographic review remains
necessary before scientific admission or any deployment recommendation.
