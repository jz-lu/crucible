Stage presentation — 16 October 2026

Default progression stages are navigation labels, independent of the existing
points policy. Curation keeps all original configuration IDs and frozen credit
weights: the old configuration selector remains the v6 weight, even when its
new stage number differs or the configuration sits between default stages.
The per-family rule remains 10 times the highest eligible configuration weight
plus 40 times the highest eligible first-record weight. No history is rewritten
and no points are earned merely by adding, hiding or renumbering milestones.
Cross-family weight comparability remains provisional and requires a separate
scoring decision; rounded default stage counts do not resolve it.

Earlier policy records follow; references to tier weights below mean the frozen
configuration weights, not the new displayed stage positions.

CRUCIBLE — PROVISIONAL POINTS POLICY v6

Policy ID: crucible-points-v6. Current eight-family ladder enrollment.

Read projections do not change this policy. The leaderboard may request
include_records=false to omit unused per-person evidence summaries; profiles may
request include_milestones=false to omit the legacy first_ten section. Both default
to true. Scores and their winning receipt identifiers are always derived from the
current database, with the same per-user family results reused within a profile
request. No participant score or recovery response is cached.

For each explicitly enrolled scheme family:
  Personal progress = 10 * highest recovered tier.
  First recovery = 40 * highest tier the participant was first to reach.
  Family total = personal progress + that single largest first-recovery bonus.
  If the participant was never first, the first-recovery bonus is zero.

For example, the first person reaching Tier20 earns 200+800=1,000 points.
Someone reaching Tier20 second, with no earlier first, earns 200 progress points.
Someone first at Tier19 and then second at Tier20 earns 200+760=960 points.
Places two through ten earn no recovery bonus. An unchanged personal best and repeated hourly runs do not
accumulate points. Reaching every intermediate tier before Tier20 earns the same
total as reaching Tier20 directly. An earlier first-recovery bonus remains when
a later personal best is not first.

Version 2 replaced v1's bonuses for places two through ten. Version 3 explicitly
enrolls all eight current scheme families. Scores are derived again from existing
evidence; no receipt, account history, admission credit or milestone is deleted.
There are no grandfathered v1 bonuses. The API retains its early_recovery field
names for compatibility. Without a positive bonus, its tier, rank and submission
identifier are null.

For DH, RSA and the elliptic-curve research release, place is determined by each
account's earliest verified receipt at that tier OR HIGHER. Their registered
inheritance rules reserve lower-tier places. For Module-LWE, FrodoKEM, NTRU Prime,
HQC and Classic McEliece research releases, place is determined ONLY at the exact
registered profile recovered. A higher tier does not imply recovery of a lower
profile or take its first-place bonus. This matches their challenge leaderboards.
Ordering is server receipt time then receipt identifier; the lower identifier
wins at identical times. Take only the highest first-place tier per family,
never the sum of first places. This rewards first recorded results while avoiding
extra points for repeated runs or visiting every intermediate profile. It
recognizes recorded account success, not independent algorithmic novelty.
Only successful, internally consistent receipts within their server deadline
qualify. JSON tier values must be genuine integers matching enrolled profiles.
Computation estimates do not change points and remain self-reported.

The frozen identifiers, ranges and inheritance rules live in crucible/scoring.py.
Registry changes never automatically enroll releases, multiply family totals,
or reinterpret archived tiers. No archived/published release is newly eligible,
even where an endpoint shares parameters with a research release. Any future
compatibility decision must explicitly define receipt mapping and deduplication.
Version 4 replaced only the module-lattice and HQC enrollment with their new
parameter ladders. Version 5 replaces the DH, RSA, EC, module, Frodo and NTRU
enrollments with the parameter-efficiency revisions listed above. Old receipts
remain archived and are not relabelled or
transferred to new tiers. Version 6 restores the Frodo640 endpoint through anchor-research-v3, with 80 natural settings. The other seven family enrollments are unchanged.
The scoring formula itself is unchanged.
The maximum recovery total is 2,500 per 50-tier family, 550 for the eleven-tier
module ladder, 4,000 for the 80-tier Frodo ladder, and 1,350 for each 27-tier NTRU
and HQC ladder. These unequal maxima remain explicitly provisional: natural
parameter counts do not determine equal difficulty, scientific value or attack
cost. The formula is unchanged; we have not inserted artificial tiers or silently
normalized points. Cross-family incentive calibration remains a future decision.
An accepted scheme contribution is separate from these recovery maxima.

Scoring presentation is derived from the current versioned policy, separately
from immutable adapter manifests. Historical manifest/public-input metadata may
contain its original disabled-scoring flag; the live catalogue and challenge's
top-level scoring_eligible/scoring_policy_id state the applicable current policy.
Frozen source hashes, manifests and existing challenge data are not rewritten.

Overall ranking sorts the sum of all component points, descending. Equal totals
share a competition rank; handles break display-order ties. Zero-score accounts
are included. Scores are an incentive convention, not security estimates or a
ranking of scientific merit. Account identity, moderation and independent-method
recognition remain future work. First ten distinct accounts do not mean ten
independent attacks.

New scheme contribution: 1,000 points once for an explicitly admitted new family.
Submission, format validation, or proposal approval alone awards nothing.
Development self-review is test-only and is rejected by independent admission
and contribution-credit gates, even if all review checkboxes pass. Disabling
the local flag later does not upgrade the recorded decision.
Trusted integration must first register the exact approved source as an active
scheme. Then a configured approving reviewer (not the proposer) records a
separate new-family admission decision using scripts/award_scheme_credit.py.
The command requires the exact proposal/review, active source/version, matching
source hashes and frozen revision, written evidence and --confirm-new-family.
Use --dry-run to inspect before recording. There is no HTTP write endpoint.
The ledger's unique family, scheme, proposal, review and source constraints
prevent duplicate retries and obvious aliases from earning more credit. Whether
a family is genuinely new remains an explicit manual review responsibility.
The proposal author receives the fixed award; existing DH/RSA examples receive
no seeded awards. Shared-author credit allocation is not implemented yet.

The ledger is additive migration0004. It does not modify accounts, sessions or
past submissions. The operator command does not execute uploaded code, activate
schemes, publish private proposals, or grant repository access.

Human interface

The headline metrics are Points and Accepted proposals. The latter counts actual
new-family admission credits, not drafts or review approvals awaiting admission.
Raw receipt counts remain in the API for compatibility but are not displayed as
achievements. Personal bests show progress per scheme; Recent solves shows the
underlying verified instances without awarding points for their quantity.

Personal records show one actual recovery per scheme. Prefer the highest actual
tier recovered in the current release. If none exists, select the most recently
participated archived release and its highest actual tier. Named published
parameter sets have no invented strength ordering: show the most recently
recovered named set in that selected release. This display projection does not
assert that exact-only lower profiles were solved, and never compares numeric
tiers between incompatible releases. The one family score appears only once.

First to reach shows one actual highest first-place receipt per enrolled family
in person.first_records. If someone was first at Tier1 and later second at Tier3,
their personal record is Tier3 and First to reach remains Tier1. Participants
with no first-place result have no entry. Places two through ten and an exhaustive
milestone history do not appear in this compact profile section.

Legacy First 10 API milestones remain available for compatibility and preserve
history, with no additional points. An actual,
valid, on-time correct submission must improve that person's highest tier in
the exact registered scheme release (or exact profile when inheritance is disabled),
and place among the first ten accounts under that release's recovery rules. Order is receipt time then receipt identifier, as in
the existing score policy. Repeats and implied lower-tier standings are not
separate milestones. The compact view keeps records not superseded by an
equal/higher tier with an equal/better place (one comparison strictly better).
Full milestone history remains accessible in pages of twenty, with original
receipt links. Comparisons never cross scheme releases. The milestone API's
counts_toward_bonus flag identifies only the first-place result supplying the
selected bonus; other First 10 milestones keep their recognition without points.
The feature records early account success; it does not certify independent
algorithms, novel attacks, independently measured compute or distinct people.

Leaderboard is the second navigation tab after Schemes. Recovered by usernames
open participant profiles. A profile shows total and component points, personal
bests with original versions, and recent verified recoveries. Individual results
open their original method/compute receipt. Incorrect answers, account IDs,
password hashes, tokens and private approval evidence are not exposed by these
participant endpoints. This uses the prototype's existing catalogue read access;
private hosting/access controls remain a separate deployment decision.
