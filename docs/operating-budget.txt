CRUCIBLE OPERATING BUDGET
=======================
Last reviewed: 2026-11-06. Currency: USD, before tax and credits.
Public rates retain their 2026-11-04 verification date below.
Planning scale: hundreds of active researchers, with occasional bursts.
Next review: first full month after screening is enabled, or 2026-12-04,
whichever comes first. Review immediately when a plan, model or workload changes.

This is the maintained home for costs, funding assumptions and upgrade triggers.
Update this file in the same PR that introduces a paid dependency or changes a
limit. Keep monthly actuals below; Git preserves previous assumptions. The release
operator updates usage, and the billing-account owner confirms invoices. Never
put credentials, private invoices, account numbers or submitted note text here.

Funding assumption
------------------
The initial planning allowance is $125-175/month ($1,500-2,100/year), including a
proposed $10/month model-screening pilot envelope. This is not an observed bill,
an enabled spending limit or authorization for additional spending. A retained
helper server and paid email would add about $44/month. Sustained growth, stronger review models or an
availability requirement should be a deliberate new budget decision.

The core baseline is approximately $77.03-92.03/month:
  $49 app + $15.15 database + $5 Spaces + $5/$20 registry + $34.50/12 domain.
Add actual CI, backup execution, model usage, optional services and tax. The range
comes from the unconfirmed registry tier, not two alternative hosting designs.
No new paid resource, model credential or deployment is created by this document.

Researchers' own agents/attack compute are outside this budget. Crucible does not
run uploaded attack code. Human scientific review, maintenance time and any future
cash prizes need separate funding; current bounty rewards are participation points.

Application: charged while the service exists. CPU/memory or replica changes
change this fixed cost. Challenge generation, HTTP traffic and background work
share its capacity; researcher count alone does not identify an upgrade need.

Database: accounts, sessions, notes, challenges, submissions, reviews and evidence
archives live here. Storage growth, connections and slow queries can require a
larger plan. Adding an app replica also increases potential DB connections. The
opt-in screening child has its own pool (default maximum five connections); its
serial loop normally uses one. Account for both old/new web and child processes
during deployment overlap, plus migration and backup connections.

Registry: CI publishes images and retains runtime/rollback images. Storage grows
with retained unique layers. Preserve the reviewed base, recovery images and
required scientific provenance before defining deletion policies.

Spaces: encrypted recovery sets consume storage and restore/download transfer.
Future large evidence blobs could use the same service, with separate access and
retention rules. They are currently in PostgreSQL; do not count a migration as done.

Email: invitation/account-recovery events consume quota; visits do not. CI:
pull requests/main checks, deliberate native releases and separate measurement
workflows consume runner time. Backup jobs charge for execution, not a permanently
running duplicate web service. Retained VMs continue billing when powered off.

Price steps and review triggers
------------------------------
These thresholds are planning prompts, not automatic upgrades or configured alerts.

* App: current $49; 2 CPU / 4 GiB $78; 2 CPU / 8 GiB $98. Two current containers
  cost $98 in app compute alone. Measure mixed-load latency, CPU/memory and 429/503
  rates first. A second app instance does not create database high availability.
  Current plan includes 400 GiB outbound; overage $0.02/GiB, pooled at team level. [1]
* DB: 1 GiB $15.15, 2 GiB $30.45, 4 GiB $60.90. Published backend connection
  allowances are 22 / 47 / 97. Code currently allows five connections per process;
  old/new deployment overlap can double app demand. Leave room for backup and
  migration connections. Review at sustained 70% disk/connection use or a forecast
  of reaching the limit within 30 days. Additional disk is $0.215/GiB/month in
  10 GiB increments. HA/Advanced needs a separate quote; availability and Standard
  edition restrictions depend on cluster/account creation date. [2,3]
* Registry: Basic includes 5 GiB, Professional 100 GiB. Extra storage is listed
  at $0.02/GiB/month on either plan; crossing 5 GiB does not force a $20 plan. [4]
* Spaces: $5 includes 250 GiB storage and 1,024 GiB outbound. Additional storage
  $0.02/GiB/month, outbound $0.01/GiB. Include recovery sets and restore exercises. [5]
* Email: Free has 3,000/month AND 100/day. Check an invitation wave against the
  daily allowance. Pro $20/month includes 50,000 with no daily cap; additional
  email is listed at $0.90/1,000. [6]
* CI: Linux 2-core $0.006/min, rounded per job. Included compute is dollar-based:
  Free $12/month; Pro/Team $18/month, shared with other account activity. Standard
  public-repository runners have different billing. Check actual repository and
  account billing before applying these numbers. Artifact storage is separate.
  A 24-runner-minute CI execution is $0.144 gross, so 100 are $14.40. The retained
  Oct 12-29 usage audit counted 4,677 rounded minutes: $28.06 gross at this rate,
  before allowance, not a monthly invoice. Review duplicate runs first. [7,8]
* Model API: review at 50% and 80% of the agreed monthly envelope. At the local
  cap, keep submitted notes and route to human review; do not buy more usage or
  retry indefinitely. These alerts are proposed, not verified as configured.

Automated first-pass note review
-------------------------------
The new expense belongs to the publication-request path, not normal saving,
reading, search, login, ratings or researchers using their own agents. A requested
exact revision creates durable work. One bounded worker call evaluates that
revision; a transient retry can create a second billable call. Approval of sharing
does not certify the mathematics, admit a scheme or award a bounty.

The implemented defaults are gpt-6-luna, a $10 per UTC month cap, at most two
attempts and one global database lease. The 128,000-token input allowance covers
the complete request. A small input fitting the conservative byte bound needs no
extra call. Larger valid notes use one provider token-count request before
generation; they are not truncated to fit. Counting and generation share a
45-second deadline. Output is capped at 1,500 tokens. The stricter application/
durable generation budget applies. Known usage settles reservations (including
unexpected overspend); unknown generation usage retains the full reserved charge.
Counting is bounded to one call per attempt but reports no billable-usage field.
This ledger records generation usage, not proof of free counting or a guaranteed
complete provider invoice. Reconcile actual billing before assuming otherwise.

Hosted execution uses an opt-in child inside the existing web service, not an
additional paid App Platform component. It still uses shared CPU/memory, its own
database connections and billable provider calls. Application mode, matching
durable control and CRUCIBLE_PUBLICATION_SCREENING_WORKER_ENABLED=true must all
permit dispatch; defaults remain disabled. The child reads only the dedicated
CRUCIBLE_PUBLICATION_SCREENING_API_KEY. Only new, explicitly consented publication
requests qualify; old requests are not backfilled. Code and local checks do not
establish hosted activation or queue progress. The operator procedure is in
docs/publication-screening-operations.txt.

On 2026-11-05, twenty fictional notes each received one live model call in an
isolated local SQLite trial. The durable ledger recorded 6,038 micro-USD
($0.006038) using known token usage and pinned policy rates for all twenty
attempts. Fourteen exact revisions published and six remained for human review.
No retries were needed. This is a policy-rate estimate for that trial, not a
provider invoice, the total November spend, a representative production average
or a statistical quality guarantee. The trial worker was disabled afterwards;
no hosted activation was performed by the trial. See
docs/publication-screening-trial-report-20261105.txt and its evidence JSON.

Cost formula, with all rates expressed per million tokens:
  per attempt = (input_tokens x input_rate + output_tokens x output_rate) / 1e6
  monthly = submitted_revisions x average_attempts x average_cost_per_attempt

Include prompt/policy/serialization in input and all billed output, including
reasoning. Reasoning is already part of the output total: do not add it twice. [10]
For varying note lengths, sum actual attempt usage rather than multiplying two
independently averaged figures. Retries, shadow evaluations and deliberate policy
rechecks are usage too. An ambiguous timeout may still be billable.

A $10 allowance therefore covers 369 / 48 / 18 full-size, two-attempt revisions,
respectively, under these assumptions; short one-attempt notes cost much less.
Quota can pause earlier because in-flight work reserves its maximum cost.

Examples assume ordinary standard processing, no cache discounts/writes, tools,
regional premium, tax or special pricing. Explicit cache writes, tools, a pricing
change or a different service tier need a revised reservation policy before use.
Do not treat the local ledger as a guarantee of the provider's final invoice.

Implemented budget controls and hosted checks still required:
* Atomic reservations, persisted usage/policy/retry identity and conservative
  unknown-usage charges are implemented and covered by disposable PostgreSQL
  checks. Confirm the final hosted runtime, grants and matching durable control.
* Reconcile the local ledger against provider billing; never log note bodies or
  API keys. The sample above does not replace monthly invoice reconciliation.
* Provider calls run outside web requests and database transactions. Durable
  leases, bounded retries and an operator disable switch preserve pending work
  across releases. Observe queue age, fallback rate and budget in the hosted run;
  HTTP health alone does not establish worker progress.
* Use a dedicated API project/credential, and provider alerts as a second signal.
  A dashboard alert is not an application-enforced spending stop.
* No extra worker hosting subscription is provisioned by the current same-service
  child. The database queue requires no Redis subscription. Measure backlog and
  shared CPU/connection impact before proposing a separately paid service.

Open confirmations
------------------
Reconcile registry tier, DB allocation, auxiliary VM inventory, billing credits,
domain renewal invoice, current CI allowance and usage. Confirm the backup/cleanup
schedules and their alerts actually run. Confirm the final screening model/policy,
cap, runtime secret, durable control and worker progress during its separately
recorded hosted rollout. The local trial and implemented supervisor are not
evidence of hosted activation. Hundreds of users remains a load-test target,
not a demonstrated capacity result.
