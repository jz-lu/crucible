CRUCIBLE — DESIGN PRINCIPLES AND DECISIONS

Parameter efficiency at each reference level — 2026-10-16

Treat a ladder as a sequence of justified parameter choices for the construction,
not an arbitrary trajectory that worsens several properties together. Declare
the cost objective or Pareto dimensions, construction invariants, candidate
domain, constraints, common correctness envelope and attack model. Search and
retain evidence for alternatives, including exclusions and any retained dominated
choices. Efficiency is conditional on that domain and model; never imply a
universal optimum from one estimator or hide a known competing attack.

Do not weaken correctness, truncate seed/message support or add padding merely
to manufacture progress. Natural intermediate settings are welcome. Unsupported
estimates remain unknown; unknown is not a low estimate or evidence of dominance.
Changing a distribution, encoding, transform or mathematical profile requires an
explicit version. Preserve all old sources, receipts, accounts and sign-ins.

Six replacement releases implement concrete improvements:
- DH7 uses explicit short exponent intervals with interval-search cost and an
  approximately 16-reference-bit design margin where the subgroup permits.
- RSA4 removes padded exponent bytes; EC curve-compressed-v2 compresses points
  on the same certified curves with the same full scalar distribution.
- Module packed-research-v3 losslessly packs coefficients in its existing eleven
  mathematical profiles; no compression error or native-message truncation.
- Frodo frontier-research-v2 includes every dimension 8..592 by 8: 74 tiers, the
  first 16 unestimated. Its restricted 128 planning target first occurs at n=592;
  this is not a claim that published Frodo640 lacks worthwhile security margin.
- NTRU3 chooses 27 profiles from 103 certified candidates under an explicit
  hybrid-inclusive classical reference and total transmitted-byte objective.
  Its first 13 profiles remain unestimated. No global or full-KEM optimum claimed.

There are eight family pages and 339 current profiles. HQC remains 27 weights with

Purpose

Help cryptographic research produce better constructions, better parameters, and
well-supported reasons to reject weak candidates. Shared scrutiny should leave
inspectable knowledge that others can reproduce and extend. Participation and
attention serve that purpose; activity alone is not evidence of security.

Priorities

- Keep the scope understandable: KEMs first, with small teaching examples before
  admission of a genuine research candidate.
- Respect attention. Present the useful next action and reveal detail when needed.
- Support mathematical arguments and concrete experiments without collapsing their
  different claims, assumptions, or evidence into one score.
- Make automation possible through exact contracts, APIs, checks, and durable data.
  More submissions must not require proportionally more unstructured review.
- Require correctness evidence and preserve the distinction among specification,
  executable conformance, observed outcomes, and confidentiality.
- Hold the declared correctness requirement fixed across each scheme's tier
  ladder. Increasing difficulty must not trade away honest agreement.
- Preserve attribution, methods, revisions, follow-ups, and intermediate results.
- Keep choices explicit, revisable, and proportional to the evidence available.

Decisions and rationale

1. Attack workflow before proposal workflow
   A proposal is useful only if others can investigate it. Build the executable
   challenge and research record first. Keep future admission requirements in the
   data model, but do not accept arbitrary uploaded implementations for execution.

2. Full shared-secret recovery for the initial numerical benchmark
   A submitted encapsulated key has a crisp verifier and records one solved
   instance. This is narrower than the weakest standard confidentiality break.
   It does not require long-term private-key recovery and does not rule out useful
   distinguishing attacks. A broader statistical track remains future work.

3. Shared hourly deadlines, independent instances
   Rounds follow UTC hours; joining late leaves less time. Independently generated
   material per account/profile/release/round limits direct copying between accounts.
   One well-formed answer is final. The server enforces receipt time, not external
   compute. Hardware, runtime, memory, methods, and precomputation are reported.
   Multiple-account participation and outages still need operational policies.

4. Numerical records and deliberate findings are separate
   Recoveries update the leaderboard without automatically filling the research
   feed. Findings can capture theory, a method, partial progress, or a documented
   unsuccessful investigation. An attached verified recovery supports only the
   narrow instance claim. The finding remains unreviewed. This separation can be
   revisited if it prevents useful work from receiving appropriate recognition.

5. Schemes and underlying problems have linked records
   Both can host theoretical and experimental findings. State the relationship
   explicitly: an attack route is not an equivalence or a security reduction.
   Compare results only when target, parameters, model, and claim are compatible.

6. Correctness is an admission requirement, not a leaderboard score
   Pin the specification, distribution, encodings, parameter profile, executable,
   and evidence. A mathematical argument, implementation checks, and observed
   failure counts support different statements. Never silently filter failures by
   regenerating instances. Policies for probabilistic correctness remain open.

7. Use a common selector with an explicit per-release mapping
   Historical parameter-bit releases use an integer n to select exact registered
   parameters within a scheme and release. The new calibrated releases use tier t,
   with physical parameter_bits retained separately, as selected above.
   Each scheme defines its size_parameter, supported integers, and parameter
   mapping. Some KEMs may support only a subset of integers or map n to a parameter
   vector. Equal n values across schemes are not comparable strength claims.
   Manifests and public challenge contracts preserve n as instance_size alongside
   the complete parameters, so the convenient selector never replaces the contract.

   Earlier extension, 2026-10-12: teaching DH release 3 and RSA release 1 support
   every integer from 20 through 128. DH n is subgroup-order bit length; RSA n is
   modulus bit length. Both output 32-byte shared secrets. These are teaching
   constructions, not security certifications; parameter size is not security bits.
   The size chooser shows five nearby supported values, adjacent-size arrows,
   a native range slider, and an exact Jump to input. The slider indexes registered
   profiles, so sparse ranges remain valid; input previews the number and nearby
   buttons, while change commits one navigation. The verified 2026-10-13 update
   leaves Jump to empty as a destination field; the rail and slider show the
   actual selection. Blank entry does nothing. Cancellation
   restores the actual selection. Its default is the next not-yet-recovered size.
   Following visual feedback, the dot marks the next supported size beyond the
   largest recovery, and earlier sizes use a lighter progress cue (now blue).
   This shading does not add accepted-recovery evidence for earlier sizes. The
   explicit recovery list and attribution remain unchanged. A left-side button
   jumps to the next unsolved target, without issuing an instance, and hides when

   space and becoming unavailable to keyboard/assistive navigation. The Largest recovered

Scoring proposal — not adopted or implemented

Readiness update, 2026-10-13: no scoring feature or final formula is in place.
Versioned recovery evidence and manual scheme review are foundations for the
discussion, not a completed incentive design. Keep deliberate analysis deferred
until resumed; the catalogue/participation update does not adopt or compute scores.

User preference, recorded 2026-10-12:
- Give profiles visible credit for successful, reproducible progress.
- Reward first successes, with much larger bonuses for early recoveries, such as
  the first ten, and for reaching previously unrecovered parameter sizes.
- Keep the proposal open for discussion; no point values or formula are agreed.

Design distinctions to preserve:
- Personal progress: a participant completes a challenge they had not completed
  before. This need not be a novel attack or a new community record.
- Community progress: an exact KEM/version/profile has its first recorded recovery,
  or a method improves an established resource/success benchmark. A larger size
  alone does not establish a methodological advance or comparable cross-KEM work.
- Reproducibility: another party can run a documented method and reproduce its
  result under stated conditions. Checking a submitted key verifies the instance
  answer, not the pseudocode, resource report, general success rate, or novelty.
- Research contribution: a theoretical result, intermediate observation, or useful
  investigation can merit credit independently of numerical leaderboard progress.

Questions to resolve before adopting scoring:
- Is a "first success" counted per participant, profile, method, or release, and
  how are repeated hourly wins limited so they cannot create unlimited points?
- Is an early bonus for the first ten accounts at each exact profile, the first
  independent methods, or something else? What prevents multiple-account farming?
- How much should timing and access to hardware count compared with methodological
  improvement, independent reproduction, and durable documentation?
- How do rewards remain meaningful across KEMs with different profile counts,
  parameter meanings, and attack costs, without making easy targets dominate?
- What constitutes a frontier, and how do revisions, corrected claims, and
  independent replication affect credit? The next larger size is not a proof of
  increased hardness, nor does solving it certify all preceding profiles.

Recommendation for discussion, not an adopted decision: begin with explicit
milestones or badges for personal progress, first recorded recoveries, and
independently reproduced methods. If a numerical score is useful, agree on its
scope, evidence requirements, caps, and bonus rules before implementing it.
