TWENTY-NOTE PUBLICATION SCREENING TRIAL
2026-11-05 | isolated local database; live model; hosted screening remains off

RESULT

Twenty unique synthetic notes were saved and explicitly submitted through the normal
application endpoints. Fourteen were automatically published. Six were kept private
and remained in the human publication review queue. All twenty outcomes and all
six expected concern-category sets matched the labels fixed before any model call.

Good-faith examples included an incorrect proof, speculative mathematics, a toy
factor-recovery program, negative results, sharp technical criticism, Japanese prose
and a report quoting a malicious instruction. Human-review examples covered an
apparent private credential, harassment, sabotage, phishing, an operative review
override hidden after a long technical body, and unrelated advertising.

All material, accounts, apparent credentials and targets are fictional. Synthetic
provenance is outside the model input, except for intentionally public toy data.
Expected labels, rationales and evaluator metadata were never sent to the model.

TIMING AND COST

Provider call: median 1.327s; p95 2.374s; maximum 3.495s.
Single isolated request: 1.679s from API submission to committed outcome.
Burst of nineteen: 28.955s from first submission to last committed outcome.
Estimated API charge at pinned policy rates: $0.006038; all 20 attempts have known usage.

A single worker processed the burst sequentially. These times include API/DB work
and small receipt-persistence overhead. The full access audit ran after the drain
and took 0.286s; that audit is excluded from the above timings.

These are local FastAPI TestClient/SQLite measurements, not hosted browser latency.
The normal idle worker adds up to its one-second polling interval. The current
visible-owner status checks use delays 2, 4, 8, 16, then 30 seconds: approximately
2, 6, 14, 30, 60 seconds from the start of a watch, plus network/render time.
Publication transitions can restart watches, and hidden views/dialogs pause them.
Consequently model/DB completion does not establish when the participant sees it.
The status-check backoff deserves a separate responsiveness pass before rollout.

ACCESS AND HUMAN REVIEW

All 20 notes remained readable by their author and configured reviewer. An ordinary
observer could read exactly the 14 automatically approved revisions, with matching
revision numbers and content hashes. The six flagged notes returned 404 to that
observer and appeared in the reviewer queue. The model did not reject any note,
admit a scheme, approve a bounty, award points, or certify mathematical content.

An independent read of all nine findings across the six held notes found their
field/line references supported and explanations faithful. No apparent token was
repeated in the explanations. This checks these outputs, not future reliability.

LIMITS

This is a successful diagnostic run on hand-selected fictional content, not a
statistical accuracy estimate, a production load test or approval to turn on
automatic publication for real notes. Human review completion has no automatic
time bound: it depends on reviewer availability.
