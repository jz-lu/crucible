Scientific distinctions retained

DH7, RSA4 and custom compressed EC have straightforward conditional-zero
agreement arguments, but custom KDFs and distributions. Their rho/NFS/generic
references are limited primitive-work models, not full-KEM confidentiality
theorems. RSA's structured primes are not uniform primes; its correctness proof
covers nonunits. The EC family identifier is historical: these are custom
short-Weierstrass curves, not fifty resized X25519 constructions.

ML-KEM release4 has degree8/16 support proofs; degree32/64 conditional bounds
2^-367 and2^-193; degree128/256 bounds2^-236 and2^-128 with an extra compression
model. Independent-noise expansion is scoped to stages3–6; independent vector
rounding only to5–6. The two historical Kyber lattice-reference models apply
only to stage6, with their own attack-model premise. No values are extrapolated
to reduced rings. Offline alternate compression choices are not current stages.

Frodo's n8 support argument requires no independent-noise premise. The n16..640
statistical result is conditional on the finite-CDF independent-noise experiment
and its concrete expansion bridge. The classical estimator covers n136..640,
while the separate quantum random-walk reference covers only n640. Theorem
sample counts and estimator sample conventions are not conflated. Published
conditional security proofs exist; exact-profile application remains unfinished.

NTRU's coefficient bound supports perfect honest agreement on every certified
tuple. That known argument is not an assumption. Its exact uniform research
sampler differs from upstream mappings. The frozen classical model is a bounded
hybrid-inclusive search with success and free-memory assumptions, not an
exhaustive security assessment. The endpoint is sntrup653, not sntrup761.

HQC needs a correction relative to the older local preview: the October29
support argument proves current stages1–4 correct for every supported draw,
without ideal-XOF or IID-noise assumptions. Their residual maxima6,15,28,45
are below the63-error guarantee. Stages5–66 keep the conditional-tail route.
Its two distinct unproved premises are finite-expansion transfer and domination
of the particular decoder-failure event. Neither asserts actual IID noise or
negligible total-variation distance. Frozen manifests retain their older
statistical wording; the stronger first-four result is supplemental evidence.

McEliece's distance argument supports unique honest decoding across the fixed
m12/t64 shortening study. The Prange support-hit proxy omits trial costs and is
not a calibrated security curve. October29's nine-tuple partial ISD screen and
six-point control ray are offline studies, not new registered configurations.
Structural distinguishers, heuristic key recovery, one-run costs and complete
success-normalized KEM attacks are different claims. No missing point is filled.

Follow-up: separate attack analysis from scheme-security obligations

The first integration placed missing full-KEM arguments in the same assumption
array as actual premises. The reader therefore displayed an "Open obligation"
badge inside the same note reached from an attack point. No graph binding
actually depended on those records, but the note ordering and shared labels
blurred the responsibilities. A successful attack owes its stated success/cost
analysis, not a proof of the KEM's confidentiality.

The nine missing-argument/assessment records now live in scheme_security_gaps,
with their existing stable IDs and scopes but no A labels. Their plain readable
sections appear beside the scheme security explanation and are explicitly titled
"Scheme security: ...". This retains the gaps for scrutiny without turning them
into requirements on an attack. Full technical history and exact old citations
remain immutable; the caller must create new revisions and repin adoption links.

The vague "informative comparison" records for DH, RSA and EC were removed from
assumptions and their graph assumption_ids. Their limitations remain in the
cost-model sections. In particular, a faster curve-specific attack could improve
the best known recovery cost without invalidating rho's own estimated cost. No
new rho mixing hypothesis, lower bound or numerical security claim was invented.

The retained attack dependencies concern the historical ML-KEM Core-SVP model,
Frodo Gaussian/BKZ/GSA/Core-SVP and quantum success/cost models, and NTRU's
nearest-plane success/rotation approximation. Their statements distinguish
heuristic dependencies from declared memory/search conventions and from claims
that no faster attack exists. The correctness premises and Frodo's scheme-level
hash-model premise remain scoped to their original sections/configurations.

The source now has 3,464 readable-body words. Source-note identities, all primary
source references, stage/profile identities and graph model IDs are unchanged;
no graph value or construction was changed. This remains an editorial scope
correction, not a fresh theorem audit or a completed formalization of every
attack-model premise.
