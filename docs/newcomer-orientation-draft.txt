CRUCIBLE: OPTIONAL ORIENTATION FOR CRYPTOGRAPHY RESEARCHERS
23 November 2026. Local candidate and follow-up ideas; not deployed.

Audience and scope
------------------
The audience knows cryptography but is new to Crucible's organization. Explain
local actions and consequences, not elementary cryptography. Keep the landing
page intact. Improve continuity inside the application and offer a short tour
of its real sections; neither a new landing pitch nor more explanatory cards
is needed. Keep the text short and specific to the screen being shown.

Structure and orientation should work together
---------------------------------------------
Visible next actions, precise links and stable context must make the product
usable without a tour. The optional tour introduces those actual affordances.
The local entry is Quick tour on Schemes. There is no new Help navigation.
No forced first-login flow. Existing deep links keep their destination unless
the participant explicitly starts the tour or follows another navigation action.
Inspect -> investigate -> record -> request a change is a useful journey
hypothesis, not a mandatory funnel or a measured description of 80% of users.
Researchers may arrive with a question, a finished note, an exact citation or an
agent already working. Choosing to contribute must not require a numerical
challenge, bounty, scheme selection or tour completion.

LOCAL CANDIDATE: short tour of four real sections
-----------------------------------------------
Exact copy is configured at the bottom of web/app.js; web/orientation.js owns
the controller. It uses existing routes and caches. There is no intent selector,
branch machinery, selected-object manipulation or agent-connection walkthrough.

1. Schemes: #challenge; highlight the catalogue heading.
Title: Find a starting point
Copy: 'Open a scheme to inspect its costs, assumptions and open questions—or
try a numerical challenge.'

2. Notes: #notebook; highlight the notebook heading.
Title: Read, then build on an idea
Copy: 'Find research by title, tag or note number. Open a note to inspect its
evidence and continue the work. Your own idea can start here too.'

3. Bounties: #bounties; highlight the bounty heading.
Title: Find a focused task
Copy: 'Bounties pair a research question with completion criteria and a review
plan. Choose one when you want a defined problem to work on.'
This stop is omitted when the bounty feature is unavailable, giving three stops.

4. My activity, Notes: #activity?kind=notes; highlight the activity tabs.
Title: Follow your contributions
Copy: 'Return to My activity for your notes, challenge attempts and bounty work.
Notes begin privately; request publication when you are ready to share.'

Back/Next navigate the real sections. Finish, Close tour, Escape or a backdrop
click exits; the current section remains available. Reopen from Quick tour on
Schemes. The tour does not create drafts, request instances, submit, publish,
allocate allowance or approve connections. It does not remember completion.
It refuses to start over an open dialog and closes on account/context change
or navigation away from its expected route. A missing highlight target does not
prevent the participant from continuing or closing the tour.

Tradeoff and possible follow-up
------------------------------
Section orientation is brief and has a small, predictable read-only surface.
It helps someone locate existing tools without depending on a particular note,
scheme or task being available. Its limitation is that seeing where sections
are may not connect the participant's intent to a first useful contribution.
The order is a tour itinerary, not a required research pipeline. In particular,
visiting Schemes first must not imply that contributing requires an attack.

A richer task-led tour is a FUTURE IDEA, not built or agreed for rollout:
choose Explore research / Bring a contribution / Try a challenge / Browse
bounties, then orient within the corresponding object and show its next action.
An arriving note or question could retain its context; an agent user could go
directly to the relevant existing handoff. Add this only if newcomer observation
shows a real gap. Do not add a branching wizard merely to cover every possibility.
Use minimal copy such as 'Write a related note or propose a change' where useful.
Definitions and consequences belong beside actual controls, not in a longer tour.

Definitions only where they matter
----------------------------------
At the stage selector: 'Stage selects parameters within this scheme.'
At challenge request: 'Your instance closes at the shared hourly deadline.'
At an exact citation: show revision and hash through the existing citation UI.
At Publish: distinguish sharing from scientific approval without a general
warning banner. At Boost: distinguish monthly allowance from earned points.
Do not frontload architecture, scoring rules or a glossary.

Capability boundary
-------------------
EXISTING BASELINE: Notes and exact citations, schemes/challenges, bounty board,
publication/proposal requests, and task-specific browser/agent workflows exist.
The initial audit used source 09958b2; a source audit is not a live-feature check.
LOCAL CHANGES IN THIS CHECKOUT: default note readers now focus on reading and
ownership actions. The Continue this research strip, Proposals & reviews, Related
notes, Record details and separate citation button were removed after user
review on 26 November. Exact citations remain on the note number. Supporting
workflows remain available to the agent interface and relevant reviewer views.
An older exact note can link to a newer published revision without silently
replacing the requested historical revision. These are continuity improvements,
not proof that contributors understand the overall workflow or that they are live.
The local Notes redesign now uses direct title-to-reader navigation, Readable /
Full text views and one optional exact-link outline. See notes-reading-design.txt.
LOCAL CANDIDATE: the section tour described above is implemented in this checkout.
FUTURE IDEA: task-led branching, selected-object orientation and a fuller agent
walkthrough. None is implemented by this draft. No extra Help menu is prescribed.

Exit, safety and accessibility criteria
--------------------------------------
- Close tour is available throughout; Back is disabled only at the first stop.
- Explicit Next may navigate to the top of a section; initial visits are untouched.
- Preserve meaningful keyboard focus and never discard a real work draft.
- Keep the highlight read-only; controls beneath it must not be auto-activated.
- Preserve usable geometry, visible focus, reduced-motion behavior and readable
  text on narrow screens and at 200% zoom. No timed progression is needed.
- Missing targets, unavailable features or changed access must leave a safe exit.
- No provider calls or writes to research, permissions, rewards or review state.

Verification boundary
---------------------
The integration pass reports automated desktop/mobile browser checks with no
application writes, 10 orientation module tests, 49 neighboring frontend checks,
and 126 asset/admission checks. These establish specific technical behavior,
not whether researchers find the tour useful. No deployment or human usability
study is claimed. Re-run relevant checks after code or copy changes.

Evaluation before adoption
--------------------------
Observe researchers new to Crucible with and without the tour. Ask them to find
a relevant note, continue its work, bring an independent contribution, and locate
the appropriate challenge or bounty action. Check that they distinguish saving,
publishing, submitting a proposal and claiming a solution. Record wrong turns,
uncertainty and whether they can resume their original task; do not infer success
from dismissing the tour. No such human study or percentage estimate is claimed.
If a common task still needs the tour to explain its primary button, improve
the underlying structure and label before adding more tutorial text.
