CRUCIBLE — ADMISSION AND COVERAGE CHALLENGE
Editorial recommendation, 14 October 2026. Read-only review; no admissions made.

Decision

The selected eight form a defensible bounded comparison set, not eight independent
security foundations or eight ready live challenge ladders. Saber is explicitly
deferred from the immediate review and trial, as examined in section 9.
Name the NTRU Prime entry Streamlined NTRU Prime, pin an exact specification,
and separate standardized parameter sets from reduced challenge parameters.
Do not add a ninth or tenth merely to fill a quota. NTRU-HPS/HRSS is a strong next
coverage candidate; it is not represented exactly by NTRU Prime. Deferring Saber
sacrifices the explicit rounding comparison and must be stated.

A useful bounded claim is: "A small KEM comparison set covering selected
traditional, lattice-based and code-based constructions, with exact challenge
implementations admitted separately." Avoid "all important crypto", "all major
assumptions", "independent alternatives", or a percentage of cryptography covered.
The site's common purpose is recovering an encapsulated shared secret from
public inputs. Common purpose does not establish equivalent security notions,
attack costs, mathematical independence, or transferred attack evidence.

1. What the existing repository actually enforces

The specification records Setup/KeyGen/Encaps/Decaps, distribution and reuse,
exact tier mapping and encodings, correctness scope, security claim/model,
references and an optional implementation hash. Shape validation checks none of
the scientific claims and executes no submitted code. Correctness may be perfect,
statistical or unknown at intake. This is a good separation to preserve.

Proposals and immutable revisions are private to the author and configured
reviewers. Existing statuses are submitted, changes_requested and approved.
Approval requires a different reviewer, the current revision/content hash,
matching implementation SHA-256, a non-unknown correctness claim, all five
review checks, and recorded evidence. Those checks are specification_complete,
correctness, implementation_conformance, input_resource_safety and reproduction.
Approval does not itself publish a proposal, register an adapter or prove security.

The trusted registry imports explicit local releases, enforces namespaced profile
IDs, and preserves old releases. Compatibility is explicit, with a stated basis
covering parameters, distributions, encodings and KDF; it is not inferred from a
shared name. That exact-release treatment should survive catalogue expansion.

A distinct operator action awards 1,000 admission points, only after the exact
approved release is active and all source/revision/reviewer checks match. The
ledger rejects duplicate family, scheme, proposal, review or source awards.
Semantic novelty remains manual. Recovery scoring is separately enrolled: current
policy v2 names only DH release 6 and RSA release 3. It does not automatically
score every registry entry. Its use of scheme_id as the scoring-family key is
sufficient for those two, but not a general identity policy.

Agent credentials currently allow challenge, method and submission operations;
proposal/reviewer routes are outside that allowlist. Future agent proposal intake
should receive an explicit, narrow scope. Never add reviewer/activation/credit
powers as an incidental extension of submission access. An agent may assemble a
review packet and reproduce tests; it must not mark itself as a human reviewer,
borrow reviewer credentials, attest that a human reviewed something, or cast a
review vote on the human's behalf without that person's explicit review action.

2. Four identities, with concrete split rules

Keep separate IDs for (a) a browsing family, (b) a canonical construction/page,
(c) an immutable implemented release and parameter profile, and (d) an incentive
lineage. An assumption record also needs its exact problem/game, distribution,
parameters, model and relationships; it is not identified just by "lattice".

SAME PAGE / VARIANT
- Parameter sets that the named specification defines within one construction;
  for example, ML-KEM-512/768/1024 are profiles of ML-KEM, not three inventions.
- Alternative supported hash instantiations or optimized implementations whose
  differences can be stated clearly inside the existing construction page.
- A conforming implementation port or bug fix: new pinned implementation/release
  where necessary, not a new scientific scheme or fresh award by default.
Even on one page, incompatible bytes, sampling, rejection or KDF behavior retain
separate release/profile identities and separate receipts.

NEW CANONICAL PAGE
- A materially distinct, fully specified construction with an identifiable target
  and useful standalone explanation: distinct transforms, assumptions, algebra,
  key/ciphertext distribution or rejection behavior can justify one.
- A materially modified experimental construction may have its own linked page,
  explicitly marked as a derivative, without inheriting the original's standard
  name, security claim or correctness bound.
- A composition/hybrid gets a separate exact construction when its combiner,
  encodings and game are specified. It links components rather than replacing
  them or claiming new foundational independence.
Page splitting is editorial, not a theorem and not automatic permission to score.

NEW SCORING LINEAGE
Require a written human decision about material new challenge work and duplication
risk. A new page, source hash, implementation language, KDF label, tier count,
standard revision or fresh setup seed is insufficient. If the same recovered
witness and inexpensive conversion solve both targets, default to one incentive
lineage, or leave the new track unscored until the duplication policy is reviewed.
A shared broad family alone is also insufficient to merge lineages: solving an
instance of ML-KEM does not solve a given FrodoKEM instance merely because both
have LWE connections. The test is practical transfer of the precise achievement,
not absence of all common mathematical techniques.

Examples:
- ML-KEM parameter sets: one page/lineage; exact profiles and distributions.
- FrodoKEM versus ML-KEM: separate pages; lattice browsing relationship; possible
  separate admitted lineages, with no claim of independent security.
- Custom Crucible RSA versus an exact standardized RSA-KEM: distinct construction
  identity; preserve existing custom receipts. Do not mint duplicate invention or
  recovery awards simply by wrapping the same RSA inversion target differently.
- DHKEM(X25519, HKDF-SHA256) versus DHKEM(P-256, HKDF-SHA256): related RFC 9180
  construction, exact groups/encodings/validation remain distinct. A parent
  DHKEM page may contain those concrete targets. Adding P-256 need not add a
  top-level catalogue row or automatic new scoring lineage.
- Streamlined NTRU Prime and NTRU LPRime are distinct KEMs, not parameter sizes of
  one unspecified "NTRU Prime" game. A parent browsing label may contain both.

Do not transfer receipts across releases unless an explicit compatibility record
establishes the relevant experiment is unchanged. Never remap archived tier
numbers into a new ladder. Current lower-tier backfill is an incentive convention
for a registered ordered ladder, not proof that each smaller instance was solved.
A new multi-parameter or non-monotone ladder must opt into any backfill only after
review; a single ranked integer is not enough to establish such an ordering.

3. Origin and credit are independent of readiness

Record construction_origin and assumption_origin separately, with values such as
previously_identified, crucible_discovery, and unresolved. Store evidence, author
attribution, earliest identifiable publication/submission, a prior-art assessment,
a reviewer and date. A known assumption supporting an original Crucible KEM is a
new construction on a known assumption. A first upload of a published paper is
an import, not a Crucible discovery. Recent admission and discovery are different.

"Crucible discovery" is a reviewed provenance claim, not proof of security or a
proof that exhaustive prior-art search is possible. Disputed origin should remain
unresolved with an explanation. Agent assistance can be credited accurately;
it must not replace accountable author attribution or fabricate independence.

The current admission ledger's "new family" needs a documented semantic rule
before more awards. Recommended interpretation: a newly admitted, non-alias
construction/incentive lineage, with a stated contribution role. Catalogue
curation alone earns no 1,000-point admission award. If a substantial implementation
and validation contribution for a previously known construction is to qualify,
record that role explicitly and obtain a policy decision; do not describe the
recipient as its inventor. An original construction may qualify without inventing
a new mathematical assumption. Existing DH/RSA seed entries receive no retroactive
credit. None of these decisions should follow automatically from the origin tab.

4. Admission gates and evidence packet

Maintain three separate public readiness states, with revision-specific evidence:

CATALOGUED
  A human checks identity, provenance, a primary specification/reference, KEM
  scope and truthful summary. Publication consent is explicit for private
  proposals. Record known limitations/attacks and whether the entry is a standard,
  research construction or historical example. A catalogue record can have no
  runnable adapter and unknown correctness; display those facts, no challenge
  button, fabricated curve or invented tier. Intake format-valid is an internal
  fact, not a public scientific validation badge.

VALIDATED IMPLEMENTATION (prefer this full label over bare "validated")
  The exact source and specification are pinned; a reviewer has reproduced
  conformance and correctness checks for an enumerated parameter domain. The
  record specifies all external dependencies and build/runtime versions, not
  merely a Python wrapper's hash. Preserve commands, test-vector versions and
  results, invalid-input behavior, randomness and distribution checks, failure/
  abort analysis, and resource limits. No implication of independent audit or
  proven confidentiality unless separately supported and scoped. Do not silently
  promote existing built-ins whose own metadata says independent review pending.

LIVE CHALLENGE
  A configured human approves the exact current revision and evidence. A trusted
  operator binds the reviewed artifact to a registry release and admitted profiles,
  and explicitly enables issuance. Validate challenge target, server verification,
  serialization, deadlines, secrecy of generated values, failure handling, profile
  suspension and resource bounds in the service. Specify any oracle access (the
  present challenge has none), key/setup reuse, and preparation versus online
  work. A scheme can be live but unscored. Scoring enrollment and admission credit
  remain later explicit decisions. Paused/withdrawn is an operational overlay;
  retain the page, reason and historical receipts rather than erase evidence.

Minimum explicit gate fields for each released review packet:
  canonical_scheme_id, parent/related identities, specification URL/version/hash;
  artifact digest plus dependencies/build recipe, adapter release, reviewer IDs;
  admitted parameter/profile list, exact distributions, encodings/KDF/rejection;
  correctness kind, bound, probability space, argument, tests and excluded domain;
  claimed security notion/model, assumption links, reduction scope and limitations;
  challenge objective/model, setup reuse, issue policy, verification and incidents;
  evidence artifacts, reproducibility commands, manual decision/time;
  catalogue publication consent; readiness state; optional score policy/lineage;
  separate construction/assumption origin and admission-credit decision.

These fields are a proposed review envelope. The existing strict specification
forbids extra fields; do not start inserting this envelope into spec-v1 uploads
without a versioned schema/API change. Current approved status means integration
review is allowed, not that all future readiness states already exist.

Automation should validate structure, pin artifacts, run isolated conformance
checks, generate comparative diffs and assemble reports. Reproduction occurs in
an explicitly isolated test/build environment, never by importing submitted code
into the live web process. A failed automatic check should prevent advancement;
a passed check should not cast the manual approval. Keep the present non-self-
review rule and exact-hash/revision binding. Material changes invalidate the
corresponding review scope rather than inheriting a prior checked box.

5. Correctness and tiering are admission issues, not later polish

The current specification can represent statistical correctness, but app.py pauses
an entire profile on any shared_secret_matches=false result, calling it an
implementation fault. That is conservative for the present perfect-correctness
adapters. Before statistically correct KEMs go live, distinguish a legitimate
honest decapsulation failure, a malformed input, a bounded setup/keygen abort,
and an implementation discrepancy. The submitter's declared bound does not by
itself prove that an observed mismatch is legitimate.

For the first extension, retaining fail-closed suspension on any mismatch is a
reasonable conservative operating rule. Call the incident "agreement failure
under review", not automatically "implementation bug". Record no challenge for
that draw, preserve nonsecret diagnostics, and let a reviewer classify it before
resumption. Do not silently reroll until agreement: that conditions the challenge
distribution and hides evidence. If success-conditioned issuance is deliberately
chosen later, specify it as part of the benchmark, quantify/report the excluded
mass and failures, and do not present its results as draws from the unconditioned
standard KEM experiment. Recovering the encapsulator's secret from one honest
public instance does not establish a general KEM correctness guarantee.

At each admitted profile, give a mathematical correctness argument or justified
bound plus implementation evidence. A finite test campaign cannot empirically
establish a failure probability such as 2^-128. Bounds must apply to the actual
samplers, all conditioning and the precise parameters; errors across many uses
also need an explicit model. Perfect correctness does not establish security.

No universal 1–50 ladder exists automatically. X25519 is a fixed curve and FIPS
203 defines three ML-KEM parameter sets, not fifty scaled versions [S1, S2].
Reducing secret entropy, changing noise/rank/modulus, substituting small curves,
or shortening outputs changes an experiment and may invalidate correctness and
security arguments. Such a challenge needs a named reduced-parameter track,
separate manifests and an explanation of what carries over. A reference entry
with only standard profiles is useful even if no grindable ladder is ready.

The one-hour cutoff is a submission window; the service does not bound total
compute, hardware, parallelism or reusable preparation. Label reported costs as
self-reported unless independently measured. One exact-secret recovery records
that instance's success. It is not a distinguishing-advantage lower bound, a
security-level measurement, a proof every lower tier falls, or evidence that a
standard parameter set was broken by attacking a modified toy variant.

6. Roster objections and a practical order

Selected eight-entry roster:
  1. Existing Crucible finite-field DH construction (custom reference).
  2. Existing Crucible RSA construction (custom reference).
  3. DHKEM(X25519, HKDF-SHA256), exact RFC 9180 KEM.
  4. ML-KEM, exact FIPS 203 version and errata disposition.
  5. FrodoKEM, pinned construction/variant specification.
  6. Streamlined NTRU Prime, pinned specification and exact profiles.
  7. HQC, pinned KEM specification/version.
  8. Classic McEliece, pinned KEM specification/version.

Why this is useful: two working small-parameter references, a concrete elliptic-
curve KEM, several deliberately different lattice design choices, and two code-
based constructions. This is editorial breadth, not quantified coverage or proof
that failure modes are independent. Our lattice/code reviews must establish each
exact entry's details; names alone do not establish their security properties.

The principal objections are substantial:
- The existing DH/RSA are custom KDF/distribution/encoding constructions. Their
  byte sizes, correctness claims and observations do not automatically describe
  standardized DH/RSA protocols. Preserve their names and implementation scope.
- "Classical" as a family heading lumps RSA inversion, finite-field CDH and
  elliptic-curve CDH together. Use it only as a broad traditional-cryptography
  tag, not an assumption family. Exact assumption tags should be visible on each
  page without adding another navigation layer.
- Three lattice entries still require explicit comparison questions, not treating
  every paper as another basis for security. Adding Saber would make four and
  nearly half of a nine-entry roster. Its deferral sacrifices a deliberate
  rounding comparison; retain that objection rather than implying redundancy.
- NTRU Prime is ambiguous and is not all of NTRU. The primary submission discusses
  Streamlined NTRU Prime and NTRU LPRime separately; NTRU-HPS/HRSS have their own
  construction and sampling choices [S3, S4]. If a ninth or tenth is justified,
  add the exact NTRU construction with its HPS/HRSS variants; do not pretend the
  Prime entry already stands in for it.
- P-256 and other RFC 9180 DHKEM suites remain outside the initial concrete target.
  They can be explicit sibling variants in the DHKEM page rather than every
  curve becoming a new headline row or new points award [S1].
- No exact post-quantum/traditional hybrid is represented. Adding two component
  pages is not testing their combiner. A future hybrid needs its own interface,
  composition assumptions and challenge target [S5]. The word "hybrid" in HPKE
  must not be mistaken for automatic post-quantum/traditional composition.
- Research on other foundations remains outside this starting slice. A complete,
  attributable KEM proposal from an unfamiliar foundation may enter catalogue
  review without standardization, fame, a tier ladder or a tenth-slot vacancy.
  Require clear claims and a safe reviewed implementation before live activation;
  do not require "independent of all existing assumptions" as an impossible gate.
- A full eight-entry live launch would be a worse scientific result than two live
  entries plus carefully labeled references if six correctness/parameter reviews
  are missing. Order implementation by review readiness and scientific value,
  not a desire to remove every empty chart at once.

The <=10 target is an attention and initial-maintenance budget, not a permanent
admission rule. Keep novel research proposals eligible, review private drafts,
and change catalogue presentation/pagination as real reviewed work accumulates.
Do not manufacture a discovery or lower standards to populate the origin tab.

7. Source quality and bounded use of evidence

Keep distinct evidence classes rather than one "verified" badge:
- Normative/primary construction: exact published standard or author specification,
  with edition, errata disposition, parameter set and artifact hash where relevant.
- Mathematical claim: primary proof/paper, precise theorem/game, assumptions and
  parameter domain; identify author claim versus independent review.
- Implementation conformance: pinned code, known-answer/negative tests, cross-
  implementation comparison, build metadata and reproducible commands.
- Empirical attack/cost evidence: exact instance/release, target, receipt, method,
  source, hardware and measured versus reported resources. Empirical evidence does
  not turn into a security lower bound through catalogue admission.
- Editorial context: classification PDF or survey, useful for navigation and
  candidates but insufficient to certify current specification, implementation,
  novelty, standard status or correctness. Search snippets are discovery aids;
  do not use a recent claim of a spectacular attack without reading and review.

8. Established-guideline audit and requirements crosswalk

Audit scope: live repository and staged work/recovery-upload were compared on
2026-10-14. The following were byte-identical in both copies: correctness-policy-
v0.txt, protocol-admission.txt, protocol-specification.txt, architecture.txt,
scoring.txt; protocol_specs.py, protocol_proposals.py, protocol_admission.py,
registry.py, scoring.py, scoring_awards.py, app.py, machine_auth.py and manage.py.
The inspected admission/proposal/boundary/calibrated-API test files also matched.
This was source and test inspection, not a new execution of the test suite.
Line references below describe that audited snapshot and may move after edits.
After this comparison, documentation-only clarifications were authorized and
staged in correctness-policy-v0.txt and protocol-admission.txt. They correct the
historical/current wording and point to this crosswalk; the audited code was not
changed and no statistical-failure policy or numeric threshold was activated.

The established guidelines are substantially sound. The proposed correctness
policy already distinguishes argument from testing, requires exact profile
coverage, forbids hidden resampling, and anticipates statistical failures. The
manual admission procedure implements important parts of those intentions.
The task now is to retain those requirements while resolving the specific gaps
below, not to replace them with a vague new checklist.

R1 — Define the honest failure event, not a generic percentage.
Existing rule/location: correctness-policy-v0.txt:9–23; protocol-specification.txt
"correctness"; protocol_specs.py:112–126.
Enforcement: the schema requires kind, bound text, argument and scope; it checks
label consistency only. It cannot establish that the probability space is right.
Judgment: KEEP; CLARIFY the exact event for each adapter.
Proposed policy: every review states F = honest Encaps/Decaps disagreement or
honest rejection, the setup/key/encapsulation random choices, conditioning, and
whether the claim averages over fresh keys or also bounds reused-key behavior.
Invalid adversarial input rejection is not automatically an honest correctness
failure. A malformed honest output is an implementation/conformance incident.
Always distinguish returned but unequal keys from an explicit error: a scheme
with implicit rejection can fail agreement without returning an error at all.

R2 — A nonblank failure bound is not evidence of a quantified guarantee.
Existing rule/location: correctness-policy-v0.txt:20–36,58–63;
protocol_specs.py:112–126; tests/test_protocol_specs.py:190–191.
Enforcement: "unproved expression" is intentionally accepted as statistical bound
text at format-validation time. Approval rejects unknown correctness, but no
numeric threshold or proof/heuristic distinction is machine-enforced.
Judgment: KEEP permissive intake; CLARIFY and strengthen the review packet.
Proposed policy: reviewer records evidence_kind = mathematical bound / heuristic
estimate / empirical result, exact formula or number, domain, assumptions, primary
argument location, known limitations, and the profile class's acceptance rationale.
Do not label an estimate a bound without qualification. Unknown or empirical-only
claims may remain catalogued/private research, but do not pass the proposed live
correctness gate merely because their JSON kind is changed to statistical.
Keep the established rule that no universal delta = 2^(-security bits) threshold
is invented. Publish a purpose-specific admission criterion before issuing a new
class of official challenges; do not silently choose one while coding.

R3 — Conformance tests support a proof; they do not replace one.
Existing rule/location: correctness-policy-v0.txt:25–41;
protocol-admission.txt:35–43; ReviewChecks in protocol_proposals.py:96–103.
Enforcement: booleans and nonempty evidence are checked; commands, outcomes and
mathematical reasoning remain manual. The service only checks one generated
round trip through adapter-provided diagnostics.
Judgment: KEEP; make review evidence inspectable.
Proposed policy: attach reproducible known-answer vectors, invalid-input and
boundary cases, deterministic reference comparisons, and honest trial counts/
failures/seeds where safe. Separately identify the correctness argument covering
all admitted parameters. A million successful trials cannot substantiate a
cryptographic-scale failure rate. A proof about pseudocode cannot establish the
compiled adapter's conformance. An independently written implementation helps
find shared-code mistakes but is not a required blanket substitute for judgment.

R4 — No correctness-conditioned retries or target substitution.
Existing rule/location: correctness-policy-v0.txt:76–83;
dh_v6.py:763–781; rsa_v3.py:327–333; app.py:479–492.
Enforcement: current generators draw once, preserve Encaps' K, compare Decaps' K,
and report one honest trial. The API records a hold and returns no instance on
mismatch. Tests test_kem.py, test_rsa_v3.py and test_boundaries.py explicitly cover
no retry/unchanged target and persistent profile holds.
Judgment: KEEP as a non-negotiable experiment rule.
Proposed policy: never replace K with Decaps' output and never rerun Encaps until
agreement. Any rejection sampling required by the published algorithm itself
must remain fully specified and must not be confused with an added service-side
filter. Report service omission/abort behavior, since a catalogue of only issued
successful-agreement instances is not an unbiased sample of all generated draws.

R5 — Mathematical failures and bugs need separate classifications.
Existing rule/location: correctness-policy-v0.txt:80–83 and architecture.txt:161–168
explicitly require this distinction. app.py:479–492 currently maps unexpected
exceptions and failed agreement to profile incidents/503s. db.py:84–91 stores a
small diagnostics object and resolved flag, not a classified correctness ledger.
Judgment: CHANGE before general statistical-correctness activation.
Proposed policy: preserve the current fail-closed behavior for existing DH/RSA.
For a new statistical KEM, review a versioned failure policy before live admission.
Use categories at least agreement_failure_under_review, confirmed_mathematical_
failure, implementation_fault and generation_abort. Classify with an independent
reference/reproducer; the author cannot label every mismatch "expected". Record
safe aggregate counts and review outcome, with private witness custody where
reproduction requires it. Never expose live secrets or a decapsulation oracle.

Two coherent future designs exist, and the present code implements neither as a
general statistical-KEM policy: (a) record and hold every mismatch for review,
with all omitted draws disclosed and experiment scope explicit; or (b) issue
reviewed valid honest draws even when Decaps disagrees, using Encaps' K as target
and recording agreement as a separate outcome. Design (b) preserves the natural
recovery distribution but requires separating generation_valid from agreement_
passed throughout issuance, ranking and scoring. The current correctness_passed
flag is a ranking eligibility filter, so simply allowing a mismatch through
issuance would still not implement (b). Do not make that change implicitly.
Recommendation for this review/trial: catalogue the statistical candidates and
leave them non-live until one policy is explicitly adopted and exercised.

R6 — Bounded key-generation aborts are neither hidden nor mislabelled.
Existing rule/location: architecture.txt:182–190; RSA3 correctness scope;
app.py:482–488; test_calibrated_api.py:test_bounded_rsa_generation_exhaustion...
Enforcement: PrimeGenerationLimit produces a retryable 503 with no challenge and
no correctness incident. Successful RSA generation has a separately conditioned
perfect-agreement claim. Other unexpected exceptions pause the profile.
Judgment: KEEP; CLARIFY the old broad "honest exchange fails" formulation.
Proposed policy: report generation availability separately from conditional
agreement. If alpha is the probability generation aborts and delta bounds failure
given successful generation, the complete exchange failure probability is at
most alpha + (1-alpha)*delta; this is not a claim that alpha is zero. State whether
encapsulation aborts are included in alpha or F, without double counting. Do not
advertise unconditional perfect exchange when only conditional agreement is
proved. Allow explicit retries of a documented generation abort; distinguish them
from repeated attempts to erase an agreement failure.

R7 — Counts need a denominator matching the claimed experiment.
Existing rule/location: correctness-policy-v0.txt:31–34,82–83 requires counts and
visible aggregate evidence; generated diagnostics say honest_trials=1.
Enforcement: issuance stores correctness_passed=True for accepted instances;
incidents record faults, but no comprehensive population-level trial/failure/
abort ledger is implemented. Concurrent duplicate requests can generate extra
draws while only one issued instance persists.
Judgment: CHANGE before displaying correctness rates.
Proposed policy: identify and count distinct generation draws, outcomes, withheld
draws and aborts per exact release/profile; do not use request count, issued
instances or successful receipts as interchangeable denominators. Publish only
safe aggregate observations with the sampling/stopping policy. Do not infer a
failure-rate estimate from the current sparse incident table. Reused-key and
independent-key campaigns must be distinguishable.

R8 — Tiers identify exact admitted profiles; unsupported interpolation is banned.
Existing rule/location: correctness-policy-v0.txt:43–63;
protocol_specs.py:64–101; protocol_admission.py:66–99.
Enforcement: lists/ranges are bounded and ordered; binding check requires the
same tier set, exact release/profile identities and bounded output lengths. It
does not prove the parameter mapping, distributions or coverage theorem match
that set. registry.py permits only explicitly declared compatibility overlaps.
Judgment: KEEP; CLARIFY names and comparison semantics.
Proposed policy: call the common integer "challenge tier"; preserve sparse sets.
Attach exact parameter vectors plus purpose (standard / reduced research) and
review coverage for every profile. One argument can cover many profiles, but a
review must identify the domain rather than infer it from endpoints. No slider
interpolation, automatic fifty-tier expansion or inherited low-tier correctness
claim. Separate ordering/points/backfill policy from security estimates and exact
standardized parameter names. Fixed output bytes are not instance security bits.

R9 — Approval needs an accountable independent reviewer, not a checked form.
Existing rule/location: protocol-admission.txt:28–49,76–92;
protocol_proposals.py:234–238,367–414; machine_auth.py:required_scope.
Enforcement: stable configured reviewer account, CSRF for browser mutations,
non-self-review, exact revision/hash, implementation digest, all five checks and
minimum evidence length. Agent tokens cannot call review routes. Distinct
accounts are enforced; distinct people or absence of conflicts are not proven.
Judgment: KEEP; CLARIFY the human attestation and limits of "independent".
Proposed policy: the named reviewer explicitly records that they inspected the
concrete argument/artifact/evidence and lists conflicts or dependence on author
supplied work. Automation may prepare or reproduce evidence, never invent that
attestation or cast the vote autonomously. Require a subject-qualified reviewer
for correctness and a responsible integration reviewer; one person may fulfill
both when qualified, with the roles recorded. Do not imply a two-person audit
when only account separation and one review occurred.

R10 — A revision invalidates approval of changed content.
Existing rule/location: protocol-admission.txt:20–26;
protocol_proposals.py:336–365,389–405; protocol_admission.py:34–57.
Enforcement: immutable revisions/content hashes, stale-write rejection,
idempotency, new revision resets to submitted, review binds current snapshot.

External-guideline verification

The established correctness framing agrees with SP 800-227 section 2.2: correctness
is an honest algorithm experiment for a parameter set, distinct from security.
Its implementation/application sections are about additional requirements, not a
claim that source review proves confidentiality. Crucible should cite this as
conceptual guidance, not claim NIST validation [S6, full PDF below].

FIPS 203 section 3.2 and Table 1 explicitly describe ML-KEM disagreement under
honest generation, conditioned on no randomness-generation failures, with stated
random-function heuristics. This supports keeping bound/estimate, probability
scope and operational aborts separate; a returned key alone does not prove honest
agreement. Standard parameters do not automatically justify a reduced ladder.
