HQC — selection and admission review
Assessment date: 2026-10-14
Decision: KEEP as one of the initial research catalogue's KEM constructions.
Live challenge admission: NOT YET APPROVED.

This is a source-based selection review, not an independent cryptographic proof,
implementation audit, benchmark, or test-vector reproduction. Recommendations
below are Crucible design judgments. Source claims retain their stated scope.

1. Why HQC earns a place

HQC supplies a useful contrast to both lattice KEMs and Classic McEliece. Its
original framework deliberately avoids hiding an efficiently decodable code in
the public matrix; it instead bases confidentiality on decisional decoding
problems for random quasi-cyclic codes. The original paper presents both Hamming
and rank-metric instantiations; they must not be conflated. [H2]

For Crucible, the marginal value is threefold: a practically motivated alternative
to lattice assumptions; statistical correctness whose analysis is an active
research question; and a concrete example of why an implementation attack, a
better generic decoder, and a changed mathematical parameter set are different
results. Those are substantial differences from merely adding another module-LWE
KEM or another set of security-category numbers.

NIST selected HQC in March 2025 after its fourth-round comparison. [H3] This is
evidence of importance and scrutiny, not a substitute for our adapter review.
Use the catalogue status "Selected for NIST standardization". The reviewed
artifact below is the team's specification, not an assertion of final FIPS
conformance. NIST's September 2026 forum message plans to incorporate the proposed
weight changes into the FIPS 207 initial public draft. [H9]

The three named profiles in Table 8/9 are:

Profile   n       message bits   w     wr=we   public key   ciphertext
HQC-1     17669   128            67     76      2241 B       4433 B
HQC-3     35851   192           101    114      4514 B       8978 B
HQC-5     57637   256           132    150      7237 B      14421 B

All use 32-byte shared keys and the default compressed 32-byte decapsulation
seed. The stated DFR bounds are respectively <2^-127, <2^-192, <2^-256. The
October revision changes weights, the default private-key format, and the GF(256)
polynomial. Section 6.2 distinguishes 2-DQCSD-P and 3-DQCSD-PT in its reduction;
"syndrome decoding is hard" alone does not identify those assumptions. [H1]

Keep these three profiles nested under one HQC construction. Preserve the
2025-08-22 construction as an explicitly dated historical release if needed;
do not relabel an old hqc-128 implementation as the October HQC-1 profile. Neither
compression experiments nor alternative decoders deserve independent front-page
slots merely because they produce another implementation or parameter vector.
They can become reviewed variants when their changed behavior matters.

Assumption entries should pin parity conditions, truncation, block weights,
sampling, public setup, and the adversary's access model. Link search attacks as
attack routes, not as an unsupported equivalence between search and decision.
This is a concrete test of whether the proposed assumption schema is precise
enough without generating an entry for every mathematical combination.

3. Correctness is statistical and model-dependent

Do not mark HQC perfectly correct. A reduction with an explicitly bounded failure
term, a model for that term, and observed test agreement are distinct evidence.
Bombar–Resch–Wiedijk show that an independence model can fail without refuting the
empirically observed concentration phenomenon. [H4] Annechini–Barenghi–Pelosi
derive a model that removes coordinate independence and report that the older
approximation was conservative. [H5] Bitzer et al. also refine dependent-error
analysis and study ciphertext compression. [H6] Read these as complementary
research inputs; do not compress them into a timeless "DFR formally verified"
badge.

Admission should record the bound, its exact probability space and parameter
revision, every modeling assumption, and the implementation's actual decoder and
tie-breaking rules. The October bound is not the older HQC-1 <2^-128 claim. A
reviewer must check that claimed bounds apply to the chosen sampling and decoder,
including any cached expanded private-key representation.

Ordinary random testing cannot establish probabilities near 2^-128. It can catch
implementation mismatches. Reduced profiles can make failures observable, but
their measured rates do not prove the full-size tail bound.

Crucible also needs an explicit issuance policy. Silently resampling whenever
honest encapsulation and decapsulation disagree changes the challenge
distribution, especially at reduced sizes. Record every generation failure and
the conditioning policy; quarantine an affected profile/round when the observed
behavior contradicts its admitted contract. A failed self-check is not a solver
achievement and must not silently create a fresh instance under the same ID.

4. Material cryptanalysis and what it does not establish

Carrier–Hatey–Luzzi–Tillich, received 2026-07-22, introduce MAGE-Stern and report
approximately three bits of time improvement for then-current HQC parameters
under their cost model, with a larger memory reduction. [H7] The HQC team cites
this work in proposing adjusted weights. Bernstein disputes the claimed
improvement and comparability of its gate/memory model in the same official
forum thread. NIST subsequently announces its intention to adopt the weight
change for draft review. [H9] Crucible should retain the competing cost-model
claims and version dates, not publish a settled universal "three bits broken"
headline. The publication supplies no one-hour full-size shared-secret recovery.

Dong–Guo's CRYPTO 2026 paper reports an attack on an official AVX2 implementation:
compiler transformations produce secret-dependent decoder behavior, enabling a
co-located Flush+Reload adversary to obtain a decryption-style oracle. The paper
reports hqc-1 key recovery with under ten seconds of online trace collection;
that is not total attack compute. [H8] It is also not public-input-only
cryptanalysis. The accompanying artifact is useful for a separate implementation
security review, not for credit on an offline recovery leaderboard without the
same access conditions.

This creates a specific acceptance gate: inspect and test the actual compiled
binary, compiler version, flags, architecture, and any mitigations. The word
"constant-time" in a source README or an earlier specification is insufficient.
Do not assume that the October document proves this issue fixed in whichever
repository revision an integrator happens to download.

5. Implementation and test-vector provenance: an actual mismatch to resolve

The official resource index links to https://gitlab.com/pqc-hqc/hqc/ . On this
review date the public tags API lists v5.0.0, commit
f46e54222ffb76706678a9ec8a43cd2188a12ab1, dated 2025-08-22; main resolves to that
same commit. The repository also exposes development branches, including
next-release. A branch name is not evidence that it implements the October
specification. [H10]

The pinned README describes reference and AVX2 implementations; unit tests, API
tests, and official KAT folders for both. These are a useful reproducibility
starting point. They have not been built or run in this review. Most importantly,
v5.0.0 test vectors cannot certify the changed 2026-10-12 construction. [H10]

Recommended sequence: catalogue the current specification; obtain or produce an
explicitly reviewed matching implementation; pin its commit and archive hash;
reproduce its exact KATs and intermediate values with a separate implementation;
check canonical encoding and malformed-input behavior; then admit a frozen
adapter. If using v5.0.0 sooner for research, publish it as the August 2025 release
with its own reviewed limitations. Never claim the two are interchangeable.

6. Full profiles versus a grindable challenge ladder

Start with the named reference profiles for inspection and measured costs. Do not
invent fifty scaled versions merely to match the current UI. HQC's finite-field
arithmetic, concatenated decoder, ambient length, and sparse weights are coupled.
A smaller n alone is not a valid parameter-mapping algorithm.

A reduced research ladder is feasible in principle, but it is a new derivative
with its own specification: admissible ambient rings; message/code dimensions;
error distributions; fixed-weight sampling; failure analysis; hash framing; and
an explicit generic-decoding estimate. Only register selected reviewed points.
If a profile cannot be validated, leave it absent. Reduced-tier progress must not
backfill a standard profile or imply its security-category claim was refuted.

For the existing challenge game, publish only public key, ciphertext, public
parameters, exact encoding/version, and deadline; verify the complete encapsulated
shared key. Do not expose decapsulation/failure/timing oracles incidentally.
Correctness experiments and implementation leakage belong in separate evidence
records with explicit access models.

7. Selection versus admission and future proposals

KEEP HQC because of its marginal research value. DEFER live activation until the
specification/source/KAT mismatch is resolved. Both the assumption and the
construction were previously identified; importing or integrating them is not a
Crucible discovery. Catalogue publication, manual implementation admission, and
the existing admitted-scheme points ledger remain separate decisions.

Future variants must explain what they change relative to this release and why
the change matters. A smaller serialization or improved decoder is useful, but
does not automatically establish a new assumption, security proof, or separately
credited scheme family.
