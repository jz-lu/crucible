FINITE-FIELD DIFFIE–HELLMAN — SELECTION REVIEW
Review date: 2026-10-14. Decision: KEEP as a local recovery benchmark.
Scope: catalogue selection and bounded source/code review, not a security audit.

WHY IT EARNS A PLACE

This is the clearest baseline for studying discrete-log attacks with shared
public parameters. It gives participants a small, inspectable construction and
lets the site distinguish preparation for a fixed group from per-instance work.
The elliptic-curve entry does not replace this experiment: finite-field and
elliptic-curve attack costs have importantly different structure. Conversely,
this entry alone would give a misleading picture of modern DH deployment.

The selection is conditional on honest naming. The present adapter is Crucible's
custom finite-field DH construction, not a claim to implement a standardized
FFDHE KEM. Its merit is an explicit research comparison, not internet market
share, a new assumption, or a new-scheme contribution award.

EXACT OBJECT UNDER REVIEW

Local scheme ID teaching-dh-kem; release 6; crucible/dh_v6.py.
SHA256: 8701162e1415ca5892308a463385bc9428c2fb06b7ad66087c6e2ca0e80fe9d3
The user-facing name need not expose the historical internal ID.

The local source specifies 50 immutable profiles with public certified primes
p, prime subgroup order q > sqrt(p), and a generator of that subgroup. Public
SHAKE-derived seeds make setup reproducible. Fresh secret exponents x and r
are independently sampled from 1..q-1. A custom SHA-256 construction binds the
profile, public parameters, public key, encapsulation and shared group value
using length framing. This is not the safe-prime distribution of RFC 7919.

Correctness follows from (g^x)^r = (g^r)^x and identical encoding/KDF inputs,
conditional on valid parameters and faithful implementation. Exact prime
certificates and subgroup checks address that condition; they do not prove
hardness. No IND-CPA or IND-CCA claim is made for the custom construction.
Variable-time Python is unsuitable as evidence of side-channel resistance.

TIERS, COMPARISONS AND FAILURE POLICY

The current tier targets run linearly from 8 to 128 nominal reference bits.
Their restricted rho/NFS model is a calibration convention, not a measured
cost or best-known-attack bound. Structured public-prime generation and shared
precomputation belong in every result's interpretation. Reusing legitimate
public parameters is permitted; hiding its cost or reusing private randomness
is not. Account for one-time preparation, amortization count, online time,
parallel hardware and total compute separately.

The standard/reference distinction must remain visible in Protocol and review
details. If a future adapter uses RFC 7919 groups and a specified standard KDF,
it is a separately pinned release/profile contract; old receipts cannot silently
become evidence about that construction. Higher-tier credit is participation
recognition, not proof that every smaller independently sampled instance fell.

VERIFICATION AND REMAINING GATES

On 2026-10-14 the existing DH-v6 and RSA-v3 focused suite passed 65 tests in
6.47 seconds. DH checks include all 50 honest round trips, parameter validation,
public setup reproduction, public-only recovery and malformed inputs. This
bounded check supports implementation regression confidence. It does not
replace independent review of generation, the KDF, randomness isolation or
the restricted cost model. See verification.txt for exact command and hashes.

Before expanding this release, require a human reviewer to record the exact
distribution, correctness argument, known limitations and any proposed mapping
change. Preserve fail-closed behavior for an unexpected honest mismatch in this
perfect-correctness construction. Do not retry away an implementation defect.

PRIMARY SOURCES AND WHAT THEY SUPPORT

1. Local source above and scripts/generate_dh_v6_parameters.py: exact object,
   distribution and explicit non-standard status. These take precedence over
   a general description of DH when describing existing challenge receipts.
2. https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7919.html (2016), especially section 5 and
   appendix A: named finite-field groups and their safe-prime setup. It is a
   contrast/reference, not this adapter's specification.
3. https://weakdh.org/ and the authors' linked paper, Imperfect Forward Secrecy:
   How Diffie-Hellman Fails in Practice (2015): practical relevance of group
   reuse and precomputation. Their TLS findings do not establish an attack on
   every profile of this local construction.
4. https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8446.html section 1.2 (2018): TLS 1.3's
   removal of static RSA and static DH key exchange. This baseline should not
   be marketed as the dominant modern TLS construction.

All source URLs checked 2026-10-14. New attacks trigger reassessment, not silent
rewriting of frozen profiles or deletion of historical evidence.
