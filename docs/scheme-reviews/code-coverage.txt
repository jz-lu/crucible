Code-based KEM coverage and exclusions
Assessment date: 2026-10-14
Purpose: justify the code-based portion of a <=10-construction initial catalogue.
This is a research-portfolio decision, not a production cryptography recommendation.

Recommended allocation: two initial construction slots, HQC and Classic McEliece.
BIKE is the strongest deferred addition. Rank-metric schemes remain an explicit
coverage gap and a priority for a future reviewed proposal; do not pretend that
two Hamming-metric constructions cover all code-based cryptography.

1. What the two selected constructions actually cover

HQC: sparse-error problems on quasi-cyclic public structure, a public auxiliary
decoder, statistical correctness, and evolving transform/parameter versions.
Classic McEliece: hidden binary Goppa decoding structure, exact honest decoding,
large-public-key/small-ciphertext tradeoffs, and current structural cryptanalysis.

These are distinct research questions even though both belong in a "Codes"
browsing folder. Their separate reviews give pinned specification dates,
implementation provenance, current cryptanalysis, and admission gates. Neither
is approved for live registration by this selection decision.

Avoid one generic "syndrome decoding" record standing in for all assumptions.
Use separate exact formulations and reviewed relationships. A Goppa distinguisher
does not automatically transfer to HQC; a better information-set decoder may be
relevant to several distributions, but its parameters and success analysis must
be checked for each one. Family membership transfers neither points nor breaks.

2. BIKE: serious merit, deferred because of marginal value and review cost

BIKE is a QC-MDPC KEM, and its official index identifies specification v5.2 dated
2024-10-10. [C1] It contributes a sparse parity-check secret and iterative
decoding: meaningful differences from HQC's public concatenated decoder, not
merely another name for the same system.

Its compact communication makes it attractive. NIST's 2025 comparison found
smaller keys/ciphertexts than HQC but preferred HQC's more mature and stable
security analysis. Decoder failures, weak-key populations, and proposed decoder
changes were central considerations. Nonselection is not proof of a practical
break. [C2]

Our reason to defer: with a small roster, HQC already establishes the first
quasi-cyclic Hamming-metric track, while Classic supplies a different structural
target and an unusually timely research case. BIKE would require another careful
decoder/failure-analysis admission before its main distinction could be measured
honestly. That work can be worthwhile, but it should displace another entry only
when we explicitly choose to study failure/reaction behavior.

There is a concrete provenance trap. The well-known awslabs/bike-kem README
identifies its implementation as v5.1, includes optional flags that change
sampling and other behavior, and contains KAT utilities. It is not automatically
a v5.2 implementation. [C3] A convenient wrapper plus passing bundled KATs proves
neither version identity nor an acceptably small DFR.

A NIST-authored study of reduced BIKE profiles observes decoder fixed points and
absorbing-set behavior. It is evidence that reduced-profile experiments can be
scientifically useful; it is not a validated extrapolation to cryptographic
tails. [C4] To promote BIKE, require an exact decoder revision, thresholds,
iteration count, weak-key policy, supported distribution, new-versus-old DFR
evidence, and source/KAT matching. No generic slider that shrinks r while leaving
all those choices implicit. A reaction-oracle experiment also needs a distinct
challenge contract from the current public-input-only key-recovery game.

3. RQC and ROLLO: distinct mathematics, not dismissed by family shorthand

Rank-metric decoding changes the meaning of error weight and introduces different
algebraic attack machinery. RQC uses ideal and Gabidulin codes; ROLLO's lineage
uses LRPC codes. The official sites expose updated 2020 submissions. [C5, C6]
These are genuine candidates for future diversity, not variants of HQC that can
be collapsed by changing a label.

Bardet et al.'s 2020 attack work substantially reduced the estimated complexity
of then-proposed ROLLO/RQC parameters and supplied replacement parameter
examples. That is a version-specific result, not a theorem that all rank-based
cryptography is broken. [C7] More recent blockwise/multi-syndrome work proposes
new generalized RQC/LRPC constructions and revisits attacks and parameters. [C8]
Therefore "rank-based schemes failed NIST" is an inadequate exclusion argument.

Defer the construction slot until we select a specific contemporary formulation
and reproducible implementation. The newest paper title, an old submission zip,
and an unrelated library implementation cannot be assembled into one candidate.
Admission needs an exact KEM rather than a PKE or CPA-only intermediate; the
field and rank metric; distribution and support sampling; decoder success
contract; current algebraic as well as combinatorial estimates; and reviewed
conversion/encoding. Determine separately whether that exact construction has
perfect or statistical correctness. Neither property transfers from the word
"rank" or from the older RQC paper.

This is the most important uncovered code-based direction. If the broader roster
has room after its strongest candidates are reviewed, investigate one such
construction before adding several slight variations of a selected scheme.

4. Other code candidates considered

NTS-KEM: do not spend another slot on it. It merged into the Classic McEliece
submission; its historical construction can be linked within that lineage. [C9]
Preserve historical version differences instead of calling old receipts current.

LEDAcrypt: defer. NIST's round-two account describes additional product structure
that enabled weak-key attacks, followed by a proposed countermeasure making the
structure closer to BIKE. [C9] An up-to-date independently reviewed proposal may
offer merit; the historic candidate alone does not justify a third related
Hamming-metric slot. Reassess a concrete revised construction on its own evidence,
not on an inherited "broken" or "safe" family label.

QC-MDPC/McEliece descendants in general: the use of another decoder, hash,
implementation language, or parameter set is not automatically a separate KEM
construction. Require a stated change in assumptions, security/correctness,
operational tradeoff, or research opportunity before promotion. Related papers
can be recorded without creating live challenge entries for every combination.

Historical weakened or intentionally broken code schemes can be valuable
reproducibility examples, but they should live in an explicitly labeled research
or historical collection. They should not inflate an initial shortlist intended
to teach the current landscape.

5. Rules this comparison contributes to future proposal review

a. Ask "What does this add relative to our closest existing construction?"
   A submission should name that neighbor and the measurable or mathematical
   distinction, rather than promise only a higher estimated bit number.

b. Preserve four independent judgments: mathematical specification complete;
   evidence/reduction reviewed within its stated model; executable adapter
   conforms and is safe to operate; publication/admission authorized. Inclusion
   for research does not assert deployability or certify claimed hardness.

c. Register actual named full-size profiles first. Add reduced ladders only
   after their distribution, correctness, and estimated attack costs are reviewed.
   Keep toy successes separate from standard-profile recovery and distinguish
   public-input attacks from side-channel or chosen-ciphertext access.

d. Canonical references, exact source revision, vector provenance, and compiler
   configuration are required before implementation activation. Prior KATs remain
   historical evidence when the construction changes. A frozen release cannot
   silently inherit an upstream main-branch update.

e. Origin is independent: these candidates are previously identified. A new
   Crucible discovery requires attributable evidence of that discovery, not a
   first catalogue entry, port, renamed variant, or newly noticed old paper.
   Review any genuinely new construction separately from its assumptions.

f. Scores do not follow automatically from this roster. New catalogue rows,
   imports, and formal variants must not multiply the existing 1000-point
   admitted-scheme award. Recovery results remain pinned to their actual profile
   and release; evidence transfer requires an explicit reviewed justification.
