CORRECTNESS BEFORE OFFICIAL CHALLENGES — PROPOSED POLICY 0.1

Status
    Correctness assurance is a design requirement for every admitted scheme and
    must guide the parameter profiles that are tested.
    Private specification intake, revision-bound manual review, and a read-only
    adapter binding check are implemented; see protocol-admission.txt. They do
    not constitute an automated correctness or security certification system.
    The numerical acceptance criteria for different profile classes, general
    handling of statistical agreement failures, and aggregate correctness
    reporting remain proposed. No universal failure threshold has been adopted.

    Reviewed against the implementation on 14 October 2026. The requirements,
    actual enforcement, and proposed changes are cross-referenced in
    scheme-reviews/admission-and-coverage-challenge.txt, sections 8 and 9.

Common requirement
    For each exact scheme version and parameter profile, define the probability
    delta of honest encapsulation/decapsulation disagreement, conditional on
    successful declared generation. State the randomness and conditioning domain.
    Honest encapsulation or decapsulation rejection counts as a correctness
    failure unless the specification separately classifies and reports it as a
    generation abort; do not silently exclude failures from the claimed domain.

    (pk, sk) = KeyGen(parameters)
    (ciphertext, K) = Encaps(parameters, pk)
    K_prime = Decaps(parameters, sk, ciphertext)
    F = key disagreement or honest rejection within the declared experiment
    correctness claim: Pr[F | no separately classified generation abort] <= delta

    Let alpha bound the probability of that separately classified generation
    abort. Overall honest exchange failure is then at most
        alpha + (1 - alpha) * delta.
    Both events and bounds must be reported; generation abort is not agreement.

    Perfect agreement claims delta=0 on the stated domain; they do not claim
    alpha=0 or unconditional exchange success. Other schemes need an explicitly stated
    bound or justified estimate with its assumptions. Label a rigorous bound,
    a heuristic estimate, and an empirical observation differently. None of
    these is a claim that an outsider cannot recover K.

Evidence required for official admission
    1. Defined object. Specification, exact parameter vector, distributions,
       encodings, failure behavior, and implementation revision are pinned.
    2. Mathematical support. Provide an argument supporting perfect correctness
       or the claimed failure rate. Review the argument, not just the submitted
       number. State assumptions and which parameter profiles it covers.
    3. Implementation checks. Test against known vectors/reference behavior;
       test honest round trips and relevant edge cases; preserve test counts,
       failures, and artifacts. An independent implementation is useful where
       available. These checks detect mistakes; they do not prove the theorem.
    4. Explicit eligibility decision. Record which profile is admitted, by which
       evidence review, and under which published admission policy version.

    Formal proofs in Lean may strengthen evidence but are not required initially.
    A million matching trials cannot establish a failure probability on the
    order of 2^-160. Conversely, a proof about the abstract scheme does not
    establish that the submitted executable implements it correctly.

Parameter design
    The common integer challenge tier maps to exact registered parameters for
    each scheme and release. State what it means, which integers are supported,
    and the complete mapping, including a parameter vector where needed. A scheme
    need not admit every integer. Every mapped profile needs correctness coverage;
    unsupported sizes must not produce interpolated or unreviewed parameters.
    Equal tiers across schemes do not imply equal strength or attack cost.
    Output-key length is a separate property of the scheme.

    Every challenge profile needs coverage by the correctness argument. A single
    argument may cover a family of profiles; do not demand redundant proofs.
    Changing dimensions, modulus, noise, compression, or decoding thresholds can
    invalidate the original argument. Recheck both correctness and how meaningful
    the recovery task remains. This constrains how challenge ladders are built.

    Record the purpose of a profile: deliberately reduced research parameters
    or the proposal's intended operating parameters. A purpose label is not a
    production-security endorsement. Do not set a universal failure threshold
    by equating it to claimed security bits. Choose and publish the admission
    criterion for the relevant profile class before issuing official challenges.
    Exact numerical acceptance thresholds remain open; do not invent one here.

Admission states and feedback
    Submitted proposals are currently private to their author and configured
    reviewers. A future public catalogue record may show pending evidence only
    through a separate publication decision and consent for private material.
    It cannot issue official ranked challenges until the required evidence and
    executable checks pass review. A reproducible correctness counterexample is
    valuable research and belongs in the scheme's record, separately from recovery wins.

    Automate format checks, deterministic test vectors, randomized round trips,
    artifact recording, and detection of changes to a reviewed version. Reserve
    mathematical judgment for the argument and its assumptions. Reuse existing
    reviewed evidence when it actually covers the new profile.

Live challenge behavior
    Use the exact admitted distribution and Encaps' K as the recovery target.
    Record decapsulation disagreement diagnostically; do not silently resample
    until it disappears. Such filtering would hide failures and alter the task.
    Keep active-instance secrets and diagnostics private. Pause and investigate
    unexpected implementation faults, while preserving the affected records.
    A genuine mathematical correctness failure is distinct from such a bug and
    must remain visible in the aggregate correctness evidence.

    The current perfect-correctness adapters fail closed on any disagreement;
    the service does not yet classify legitimate statistical failures or maintain
    a complete correctness-rate denominator. Accepting a statistical claim at
    intake or recording manual approval does not implement that missing policy.
    Before a statistical candidate becomes live, explicitly review the failure
    event, generation-abort conditioning, issuance behavior and aggregate evidence.
    Never silently turn successful-agreement filtering into the stated original
    distribution. See the dated crosswalk, requirements R1–R8.

Current reference implementations — 14 October 2026
    Finite-field DH release 6 and RSA release 3 each register tiers 1 through 50.
    Each tier maps to exact parameters under its frozen initial classical
    reference model; it is not the subgroup/modulus bit length or a measured
    security level. Both constructions produce a 32-byte shared secret.

    DH6 uses pinned certified prime fields and prime-order subgroups. RSA3
    generates fresh balanced certified factors from the documented structured
    distribution, with bounded generation. Their algebraic arguments establish
    perfect honest agreement under the specified arithmetic and encoding; RSA's
    claim is conditioned on successful bounded generation. Generation exhaustion
    returns no instance and can be retried explicitly, separately from agreement.
    Known-answer, boundary, validation and round-trip tests check implementation
    behavior. A disagreement or unexpected fault pauses the profile without
    correctness-conditioned resampling.

    These checks do not constitute independent admission review or certify
    confidentiality. The custom KDFs, distributions and encodings do not turn
    these examples into standardized deployment KEMs. General statistical-KEM
    admission remains pending the failure and evidence policy above.

Historical note
    The original implementation section described DH release 2: 17 sizes from
    20 through 36, labeled by subgroup-order bit length. Release 1's five profiles
    and KDF domain were retained unchanged in that release. Those historical
    releases and receipts remain identified by their original parameters; their
    old size numbers must not be reinterpreted as current challenge tiers.
