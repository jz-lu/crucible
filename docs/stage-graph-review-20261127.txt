Scope of evidence

Inspected all eight local release descriptions, default progressions, profile
metrics and note-backed graph models. Matched all 377 default-stage parameter
sets and both encoded sizes to a retained native resource batch, run 40770844682.
Matching parameters and sizes is useful identity evidence; it does not prove
that an old report is currently active on the hosted website. The isolated
preview database has no activated resource publications. No hosted reports
were audited or activated, and no timing runs were started during this pass.

The retained reports use serialized-native-honest-v3. All chosen operation
series are complete: 100 recorded trials, five processes, twenty measured
exchanges per process, three warmups plus one separately retained first call.
The recorded contract includes RNG, validation/import, serialization, KDF and
internal allocation in the operation; startup, initial setup and caller buffer
allocation are excluded. Agreement checks happen outside timing. A complete
series cannot discard unsuccessful calls to improve its reported number.

The current native_v2 source was inspected as supporting implementation context,
not mistaken for the exact v3 source closure of the historical run. The v3 method,
sampling contract and statistics were read from the retained report itself.
Independent source-closure reproduction remains outstanding.

Three CPU families appear in this batch:
- AMD EPYC 7763: module lattice, Frodo, HQC, elliptic curves.
- AMD EPYC 9V45: McEliece, RSA.
- Intel Xeon Platinum 8370C: finite-field DH, Streamlined NTRU Prime.
Each plotted within-scheme series has one comparison-profile identity, CPU model
and implementation hash. A matching CPU model does not prove constant frequency,
identical host load or absence of drift. Cross-scheme timing rankings are not
justified by this batch. A standardized rerun should use one declared machine
class, controlled load and a recorded build/operation contract.

What the numbers currently say

Encoded public-key and ciphertext sizes never decrease along any of the eight
default routes. Flat size is legitimate: the McEliece path keeps its syndrome
length at 96 bytes because m*t remains 768.

Measured runtime need not rise at every stage. Some curves deserve investigation:
- DH 49 → 50: encapsulation 8.114 ms → 4.359 ms, with comparable decreases in keygen
  and decapsulation. Five worker medians cluster on each side of the drop.
- EC 24 → 25: encapsulation 510.27 µs → 348.11 µs, with comparable other-operation
  decreases. This also exceeds the within-configuration worker variation.
- Frodo's largest adjacent decrease in the inspected series is under 1%.
- McEliece encapsulation is 62.41 µs at Stage 1 and 36.44 µs at Stage 50. Its fixed-
  weight sampler has a reason to get cheaper as n increases: the earlier joint-
  parameter review computes about 11.74 expected batches at n = 840 and 1.79 at n = 3488.
  This is a plausible mechanism documented independently of the timing shape;
  it is not proof that it explains every observed dip. Stochastic key generation
  and encapsulation also have substantial worker-to-worker variation.

Do not smooth these curves or remove configurations merely to make them rise.
For the large DH/EC discontinuities, reproduce just the adjacent configurations
first, interleaving their order across independent processes. Inspect arithmetic
backend thresholds, compile differences and environmental drift. Those are
hypotheses, not established causes. Retain unsuccessful attempts, cold calls and
raw samples. Quartiles are not confidence intervals. No new error bars are
proposed for the main UI.

Attack evidence needs its own meaning

The local note-backed common-unit curves currently cover DH, RSA and EC only.
They are explicit recovery baselines (interval BSGS, trial division, curve BSGS),
with scoped >=50% success, in the declared bit-RAM model. They are not best-known
attack costs and are not security lower bounds. In particular, a large
trial-division work bound is not a useful claim that factoring is that hard.

Historical reference figures remain separate: ML has a classical and quantum
endpoint reference, Frodo has 64 classical and one quantum point, NTRU has 12
classical points, and the older classical DH/RSA/EC references cover 50 each.
HQC and McEliece have no numeric reference on the inspected current route.
Missing values stay missing. No line is interpolated through unknown estimates.
The historical models are explicitly incomplete; their units are not silently
converted into a common security scale.

Design implication: distinguish measured runtime, exact encoded size, and a
specified attack estimate, but communicate this with short titles and captions.
A simple graph cannot fix an inappropriate scientific interpretation. I would
not title a baseline-attack curve simply 'Security'.

Scheme-by-scheme disposition

1. Module-lattice encryption — retain six stages provisionally.
   Degree 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256 at fixed rank 2, q = 3329, noise and compression settings.
   The last point is the ML-KEM-512 endpoint. The smaller rings are custom
   research constructions, with different algebraic structure; the endpoint's
   security assessment does not transfer to them. The common correctness target
   is a ceiling under declared premises, not an identical failure rate or uniform
   proof strength. This is a simple authentic restricted path. Adding arbitrary
   intermediate degrees to make fifty stages would make it worse. Improve attack
   coverage and the explanation of six uneven jumps before changing the route.

2. FrodoKEM — retain eighty stages provisionally.
   n = 8…640 in steps of 8 preserves the endpoint's noise/compression settings. The eighty
   settings are natural dimension increments, not eighty equal security steps.
   The timing path is comparatively smooth. Fixed costs can make small settings
   inefficient without making the progression invalid. Check the scoped
   correctness model and attack references independently before labeling the
   whole route with comparable bits of security.

3. Streamlined NTRU Prime — retain 25 selected stages, review coverage.
   There are 27 registered configurations; two sit outside the default route.
   Polynomial degree, modulus and weight move together. This finite selection
   avoids some dominated candidates within its stated screen; it does not prove
   a global optimum. Only 12 default points have the inspected historical hybrid
   attack reference. Preserve those gaps and explain the finite selection.

4. HQC — prioritize scientific/model review, not cosmetic resampling.
   Sixty-six natural weight settings, with code sizes selected under the declared
   decoder/noise model. The endpoint is the historical August 2025 construction.
   The repository explicitly records unresolved contemporary reassessment.
   This pass did not independently re-evaluate that literature. Do not describe
   this endpoint as a newly established 128-bit target or conflate the
   correctness surrogate with a proved bound for the real distribution.

5. Classic McEliece — highest priority for a replacement-route study.
   Fifty shortened lengths 840…3488, m = 12 / t = 64 fixed. This is a legitimate
   shortening experiment, but a poor candidate for claiming parameter efficiency
   at small security levels. The earlier joint study is the correct starting
   point: it considers m,n,t together while flagging structural-attack coverage
   and the absence of a reviewed generalized native implementation. Do not turn
   its attractive diagnostic tuples directly into live stages. Flat ciphertext
   and falling encapsulation are not themselves defects.

6. Finite-field DH — retain temporarily, investigate timing and attack scope.
   Fifty configurations reaching a 3072-bit field, with an explicit shortened
   secret-exponent distribution. Short exponents can be an efficiency choice,
   but the current margin and groups are custom. Fixed-group precomputation and
   specialized structure belong in the attack analysis. The large timing drops
   need targeted reproduction; they do not justify changing the parameters.

7. RSA — retain temporarily, improve attack interpretation and timing evidence.
   Fifty balanced-factor modulus sizes, ending at 3072 bits. A straightforward
   structural control, selected against an incomplete classical reference.
   The three-byte public exponent is an encoding decision. Trial division is
   an auditable baseline, not the best-known factoring attack. No attempt here
   to certify the existing prime distribution or custom KDF.

8. Elliptic-curve DH — retain temporarily, prioritize curve-specific review.
   Fifty distinct certified prime-order research curves, reaching a 257-bit group
   order. They are not resized X25519. Compression is lossless but introduces
   decoding work. Generic rho/BSGS analysis alone does not establish that every
   chosen curve lacks a cheaper special attack. Investigate 24 → 25 timings while
   preserving the exact curve identities.

What should happen next

First workshop the local graph layout and amount of explanation. Then investigate
specific timing anomalies, using one controlled environment. For substantive
stage changes, start with McEliece's joint parameters and HQC's model/endpoint
assessment, each as a separate scientific proposal supported by exact notes.
Do not undertake a mass relabeling or wipe scores/records.

Any changed progression must identify what changed: route membership/order only,
implementation only, or the actual protocol/parameters/distributions. Preserve
old configurations, note pins, receipts and historical charts. New scientific
claims need their own review; new bytes/runtime identities need fresh evidence.
Recoveries do not transfer merely because new settings have similar stage labels.
