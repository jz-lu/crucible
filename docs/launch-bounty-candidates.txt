A. Build key exchange on a new mathematical foundation
-----------------------------------------------------
Public summary
  Find a promising mathematical problem, formulate the assumption precisely,
  and show how it can support a new key-exchange construction. The goal is a
  credible new direction for cryptography that other researchers can test.

Why this is worthwhile
  Diversity of mathematical foundations is an established objective. NIST's
  explanation of selecting HQC explicitly describes the value of a different
  mathematical approach alongside ML-KEM [1]. That supports the motivation;
  it does not establish the merit or security of any particular new proposal.

Proposed completion criteria
  Deliver a complete distributional problem and assumption, a substantive
  comparison with the closest prior work, a concrete key-exchange/KEM design,
  a correctness argument and a conditional security reduction with quantified
  losses. Include a reference implementation, reproducible small-parameter
  experiments and an initial analysis of natural generic/structural attacks.
  Identify the finite-parameter claims, unproved premises and useful research
  parameter sets. Supply test vectors and exact artifacts another person can
  independently run. A new name for an existing assumption does not qualify.

Verification
  An independent cryptographer assesses novelty, whether the assumption is
  meaningful rather than a restatement of the desired scheme's security, and
  whether the claimed implication follows. Reproduce the implementation and
  experiments and examine obvious reductions to existing easy/hard problems.
  A positive verdict accepts a research contribution, not production security.
  Scientific novelty and plausibility require judgment; they are not fully
  machine-verifiable. This is a difficult, potentially long-running task.

B. Cut the data needed for post-quantum key exchange in half
----------------------------------------------------------
Public summary
  Develop a key-exchange construction that uses at most half the public data
  of ML-KEM-512 while pursuing the same security target and a practical cost.
  Make the tradeoff explicit and provide evidence others can reproduce.

Why this is worthwhile
  FIPS 203 specifies an 800-byte encapsulation key and 768-byte ciphertext for
  ML-KEM-512 [2]. This gives a stable 1,568-byte single-exchange comparison and
  a proposed 784-byte target. These numbers alone do not establish equal
  security between different schemes. The factor of two is a proposed stretch
  objective, not a claim that such an improvement is currently achievable.

Proposed completion criteria
  Provide a complete KEM and canonical encoding with public key plus ciphertext
  plus any per-exchange setup at most 784 bytes. Count required shared data;
  no uncharged trusted setup, cached peer key, key reuse or batch amortization.
  Target the same declared NIST category-1 threat level, including quantum
  attacks, and an honest agreement failure bound at most 2^-128 under explicit
  premises. Supply a conditional IND-CCA security argument, the strongest
  applicable attack analyses found, reference code and exact test vectors.
  Do not infer category-1 security from a Crucible classical baseline curve.

  Proposed practical-cost constraint: no operation's warm median or measured
  process-memory peak exceeds twice that of the agreed ML-KEM-512 baseline
  under the same measurement plan, hardware, build and operation boundaries.
  Separate operation timings; do not average away a slow decapsulation step.
  A genuine construction/encoding improvement is required; merely deleting
  validation or suppressing failures is not an improvement.

Verification
  Check byte lengths and successful/invalid-input behavior independently;
  rerun comparative measurements from raw samples; review the complete proof,
  correctness calculation and attack analysis. Distinguish conditional
  evidence from a guarantee against unknown attacks. Before admitting this
  bounty, freeze the baseline implementation/report hashes and exact resource
  constraints in its terms. This task is auditable but very ambitious. If its
  target is too strong, change it before funding, not after submissions arrive.

C. Find an assumption that fails
-------------------------------
Public summary
  Find compelling, reproducible evidence that an explicit assumption in Crucible
  does not hold. Show what goes wrong, where the assumption is used, and what
  the finding changes about the construction or its claimed guarantees.

Why this is worthwhile
  Useful cryptographic research includes discovering the limits of a model.
  Work on correlated noise in code-based systems provides a real example of
  evidence contradicting a modeling assumption without proving the entire
  cryptosystem insecure [4]. Our task concerns an exact assumption recorded
  in Crucible; historical criticism of another model is not automatically a
  counterexample to a current statement.

Proposed completion criteria
  Identify the exact note ID, revision, hash and statement ID. Explain the
  quantified claim, distributions, admissible parameter range and the specific
  consequence the evidence contradicts. Supply either:

  - A mathematical counterexample or exact finite computation that violates
    the claimed statement inside its declared scope; or
  - Compelling experimental evidence against a stated probabilistic prediction,
    with a justified sampling method, raw data, independently rerunnable code,
    uncertainty analysis and independent replication. State the statistical
    strength and limitations. Do not describe statistical evidence as a
    deductive proof of falsity.

  Account for selection effects, repeated testing, correlations and the full
  failure event when relevant. Freeze the tested hypothesis and experimental
  plan before the confirmation run. For a tiny failure-probability claim,
  observing no failures does not verify it; observed failures require analysis
  under the exact claimed distribution rather than anecdote.

  Trace the result into at least one actual use of that assumption: which
  argument loses support, which parameter range is affected, and what still
  follows independently. Propose the narrow correction or withdrawal warranted
  by the evidence. A successful contribution need not repair the construction
  or settle its full security. The result must add a material finding not
  already recorded as resolved in the target note's history.

Verification
  An independent reviewer reproduces the exact counterexample or confirmation
  experiment, checks scope and probability conventions, and assesses whether
  the evidence genuinely contradicts the recorded assumption. Publish the
  evidence as an owned research note with exact pins and reproducible artifacts.
  A counterexample to a stronger unstated claim, an out-of-scope toy example,
  a worse upper bound, a coding bug unrelated to the stated mathematical model,
  or disagreement with a label is insufficient.

  A bounty award records acceptance of this contribution. Updating the displayed
  assumption/argument follows the ordinary correction workflow; evidence must
  remain distinguishable from that subsequent decision. Experiments use public
  mathematical objects and controlled local fixtures, with no live interception
  or third-party credentials needed.

D. Improve a meaningful attack-cost curve by at least 10x — reserve
----------------------------------------------------------------
Public summary
  Find a substantially better way to test a research construction's security,
  and turn the result into a reproducible attack-cost curve.

Vetting decision
  Do not publish this as a generic 'beat the current graph' task. Current
  Crucible graphs include exhaustive enumeration and RSA trial division.
  Beating those by ten is not necessarily a new cryptanalytic result.

  An admissible version must fix the exact research construction/configurations,
  a reviewed competitive baseline, success probability, classical/quantum
  computation model, memory budget and preprocessing convention. The goal is
  W_new <= W_baseline/10 at the frozen targets: a decrease of log2(10), about
  3.322, on a log2-work axis. It must recover the same complete target with the
  same success requirement. Merely moving an upper-bound curve upward provides
  no evidence of improved security. No live-service access or interception is
  required; submitted evidence is mathematical analysis and controlled fixtures.

  A concrete baseline and novelty review are still needed. This is not ready
  for admission or a claim of a funded breakthrough opportunity.
