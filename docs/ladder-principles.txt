Configuration and stage design — accepted direction, 16 October 2026

The scheme defines a mathematical construction and its admissible configurations.
A configuration is an exact parameter set, or the output of a fully specified
reviewed generation rule within a declared domain. It has a stable identity.
A challenge instance is one freshly sampled public key/ciphertext at that
configuration. These are different objects.

The default progression is the ordered path through reviewed configurations that
people see first. A stage is a configuration's ordinal position on that versioned
path. Stages help navigation; they are neither security bits nor a universal unit
of difficulty. Different schemes can have different numbers of stages. Include
all useful natural configurations where appropriate. Prefer a default-stage count
in multiples of ten, or five when that fits the natural settings better, when a
useful route can be selected without changing the cryptography. This is a human
presentation preference, not an admissibility requirement or a fixed 50 target.
Keep reviewed configurations omitted from the default route accessible as optional
settings; do not lose their identities, evidence or recovery credit. A scheme can
describe alternative tradeoff paths without multiplying catalogue entries.

Principles for constructing and reviewing a progression

1. Identity first. Declare the mathematical family, complete distributions, KEM
   transform, challenge game and shared setup. Preserve the intended construction
   as faithfully as possible. Dimension, modulus, noise and compression may need
   coordinated adjustment. A changed distribution or transform needs explicit
   justification; a favorable estimator result alone is insufficient. Short-
   exponent DH remains an explicit variant requiring review under this principle,
   not a neutral encoding change.

2. Honest scaling. Use the declared construction rule at natural parameter
   settings. Do not introduce specially easy keys, leaked bits, artificially
   restricted randomness or selective resampling to manufacture reachable stages.
   A genuinely smaller native input space may be inherent; disclose when generic
   enumeration becomes the best attack. Small-parameter attacks need not
   extrapolate to larger instances. Small-instance recovery alone is not evidence
   against the published endpoint or its security assumption.

3. Stable correctness contract. Choose and justify a claimed common failure upper
   bound, with explicit assumptions for every admitted configuration, including any
   off-route configuration. Zero failure is allowed, not required; equal actual
   probabilities are not required. Publish stronger individual bounds and material
   variation, rather than conceal them under a loose envelope. Keep availability
   and resource aborts separate. Do not silently change perfect to statistical
   correctness within a frozen release. New approval/admission requires a claimed
   common failure bound at most 2^-128, or perfect agreement. Meaningful explicit
   conjectural assumptions are allowed; weaker or unknown claims remain drafts.
   This target is not unconditional certification; see correctness-policy-v1.txt.
   Bounds, evidence models and their scope require substantive review.

4. Defensible efficiency. Specify workload and communication/computation/memory
   objectives. Reject demonstrably inferior choices within the declared family,
   correctness contract and attack model, allowing justified reference anchors.
   Several tradeoffs may be legitimate. Finite searches and incomplete models do
   not establish global optimality. Fidelity comes before optimization of a proxy.
   Increased cost is a real tradeoff, not a goal to engineer into every step.

5. Reference endpoint and stable evidence. Retain a published or approved reference
   configuration where one exists; new proposals justify their own endpoint and
   claims. Record exact configuration identity, progression revision and security
   evidence revision separately. An improved estimate does not change the sampled
   problem. Inserting a stage cannot rewrite configuration identities or past
   receipts. A new distribution, transform or encoding needs a distinct executable
   release. Preserve fixed instances, source identity and historical evidence.
   The Frodo plot's old 128 ceiling is not evidence that 640 is dominated by 592.

6. Useful natural progression. Choose steps after defining the family, guarantee
   and cost rules. Favor meaningful increments and measure approachable settings
   where possible. Neither equal counts nor linear security spacing is required.
   Natural settings can still be coarse; report gaps and uncertainty rather than
   fill them with artificial problems or invented solve-time estimates. A default
   progression need not exhaust all admissible configurations. Conversely, useful
   natural configurations need not be hidden merely to reduce the stage count.

Construction sequence

  Define the family, reference endpoint, invariants and challenge game.
  Declare the common correctness envelope and resource objectives.
  Enumerate admissible configurations or specify a reviewed generation domain.
  Compare relevant attack and resource evidence, preserving uncertainty.
  Select a justified default progression and explain any useful alternatives.
  Assign stage ordinals and publish exact mappings, identities and revisions.

Proposal and implementation scope

These recommendations belong in the existing proposal fields and manual review:
parameters.mapping/distribution/reuse, tier_design.parameter_progression,
fixed_correctness, intermediate_steps, endpoint fields and parameter_selection.
The v1 wire names tier_design, supported_tiers, tier_meaning and instance_size
remain for compatibility. supported_tiers/instance_size retain their legacy
numeric configuration-selector meaning; they need not equal the curated stage
count or stage position. Record the default stage-to-configuration mapping
separately. User-facing language is stage. Relabeling never changes a distribution.

Existing review checks cover specification completeness, stage design, parameter
selection, correctness and implementation conformance. They require substantive
human evidence; text validation cannot certify a natural progression or proof.
Historical documents, accepted revisions and frozen manifests retain their bytes
and hashes. New review must assess the current guidance; no additional automatic
mathematical certification is claimed.

The current service still admits trusted registered release/profile sets. This
contract does not add automatic execution of generation rules, an arbitrary
configuration explorer or an independent progression-edit API. Those mechanisms
need deliberate implementation and review. Current stage counts reflect each
registered progression; legacy 50-point paths are not mathematical necessities.

Scoring caveat

Ordinal recovery points remain provisional. Adding intermediate configurations
must not manufacture scientific value or justify a larger award for the same
recovery. A future score policy should be independent of arbitrary route density. For this
curation pass, existing configuration credit stays fixed even when its displayed
stage changes; no replacement formula or retrospective score migration is adopted.
