Explore mathematics for a potentially new KEM assumption

Read the final "Current research constraints" section before starting. Its
scalability gate and prohibition on concerted attacks supersede the original
exploration stages below.

You are conducting open-ended mathematical research for Crucible, a platform
for proposing and testing key-encapsulation mechanisms (KEMs). Seek a promising
source of average-case computational hardness that could support a KEM and has
no obvious substantive equivalence to an established cryptographic assumption.
Prefer ideas with potential resistance to quantum attacks, while treating that
as a question to investigate. Your task is discovery and critical evaluation;
you do not have to produce a successful construction.

Give yourself room to explore. The stages below are a suggested research rhythm,
not a rigid schedule. Follow unexpected connections, revisit discarded ideas
when new evidence warrants it, combine approaches, and change direction. Spend
longer where the mathematics becomes interesting. You may interleave stages or
replace an unhelpful technique. Keep the KEM objective in view without forcing
every early observation into a construction.

1. Begin with mathematics, before a cryptographic taxonomy.

Choose mathematical objects, phenomena, or constructions that you find
structurally interesting. Read mathematical sources, work through examples,
prove small lemmas, and run exploratory computations. Let the objects suggest
the questions. Do not begin by enumerating familiar cryptographic families and
making variations of each, or by producing a fixed quota of candidate schemes.

Possible questions, when useful: what information does a transformation retain
or erase? What can someone compute with an extra witness or representation that
appears difficult without it? Can independent choices produce a common hidden
quantity? Are there distributions that are easy to generate but difficult to
analyze from their public description? These are invitations, not an exhaustive
menu. An unexpected mechanism is welcome.

Allow an initial exploration before a systematic cryptographic novelty audit.
Use existing mathematics freely. Do not ignore a known connection once you see
it, but do not abandon an idea merely because its vocabulary resembles an
existing field. Record concise observations, experiments, and reasons to pursue
or reject directions; avoid a polished story unsupported by actual work.

2. Make a promising mechanism concrete.

When a direction earns further attention, define its objects, parameter family,
efficient sampling procedure, public distribution, secret information, and the
precise computational task facing an adversary. Explain what creates useful
asymmetry between legitimate participants and an outsider. A difficult search
problem alone does not give a KEM, and a trapdoor permutation is not required.

Try to specify KeyGen, Encaps, and Decaps, including randomness, encodings, output
lengths, and failure behavior. Explain why independently run honest algorithms
agree on the shared secret. Prove correctness where possible; otherwise isolate
the missing statement and measure failures without silently discarding them.
Use small executable examples when they clarify the mechanism. State any gap
between a useful mathematical observation and a complete construction.

3. Try seriously to destroy the idea.

Look for public invariants, unintended leakage, simpler representations, cheap
recovery methods, and statistical distinctions. Consider preprocessing and
repeated instances as well as single-instance attacks. Investigate applicable
classical and quantum algorithms. Let the candidate's structure determine the
attacks instead of treating a generic checklist as evidence of security.

Separate honest correctness, difficulty of recovering the secret, and security
of the encapsulated key against distinguishing attacks. State the adversary's
access and the claimed security game. Treat IND-CPA and IND-CCA as distinct
targets; account for every assumption needed by a security transformation.
Hashing a hidden value does not by itself establish a secure KEM.

Report successful attacks, weak parameter regimes, and negative results. Tests
on small instances can expose defects; surviving them does not justify a
claimed number of security bits. A partial mechanism with a precise obstacle
is a useful outcome.

4. Audit novelty once there is something substantive to compare.

Search primary literature using both the mathematical structure and the proposed
security problem, including older or broken proposals. Identify the nearest
known constructions and attempt concrete translations, reductions, and attacks.

Distinguish a renamed known problem, a meaningful variant, and a potentially new
source of hardness. A generic encoding as polynomial equations, or a generic
implication shared by all KEMs, is not enough to establish substantive sameness.
Explain whether a proposed connection preserves the sampled distributions,
parameters, secret-assisted mechanism, or relevant attack cost. Do not claim
proof of independence from all known assumptions. Say what connections you
searched for, found, or could not rule out, with verifiable sources.

5. Leave a research result someone else can inspect and continue.

For any surviving candidate, provide a clear intuition, mathematical definition,
algorithms or explicit missing mechanism, correctness evidence, security claims
and gaps, attack results, novelty comparison, and reproducible code where useful.
Suggest the next experiment most likely to resolve an important uncertainty.
Keep proven statements, conjectures, measurements, and heuristic estimates
visibly distinct. Do not invent citations or assume worst-case hardness implies
hardness on the generated instances.

Choose the output format that best explains the work. When saving a notebook note,
use its title and dropdown summary to orient a human reader; keep the body free-form
for agents and expert reviewers. Dense LaTeX, proofs, calculations and code are
welcome. No fixed internal sections or general-audience prose are required; use
whatever structure the research needs and preserve precise citations and limits.
A short account of failed
directions and a valuable lemma may be better than an elaborate weak proposal.
If nothing survives, say so. The objective is a new, inspectable research lead
with a credible path toward a KEM, not novelty in terminology or a compulsory
claim of success.

Current research constraints (2026-10-14; supersede conflicting text above)

Restart from square one. The next search should begin with a new mathematical
mechanism, not presume that the existing boundary-action construction is to be
repaired. Preserve that candidate and its evidence in the research index at
kem-discovery/research-index.txt. Carry forward the constraints below rather
than the old construction as a default architecture.

Scalability is a prerequisite for further experimental development. Before
stress testing or implementing a new candidate, describe a parameter family
indexed by a target security parameter lambda, its exact public distribution,
and the proposed relationship between that parameter and computational security.
Here lambda denotes intended security bits: the target is work exponential in
lambda, not merely a key or secret containing lambda bits. A new assumption may
remain conjectural, but its parameter dependence must be stated and motivated.
Do not label a dimension, field size, secret entropy or hash length as achieved
security. No proof or experimental security estimate may be invented.

Derive a small-polynomial upper bound on the full encoded public-key size as a
function of lambda, counting required public parameters, bases, generators,
metadata and the bit width of field elements. The working design target is
quadratic growth, with all logarithmic factors stated. Cubic growth requires
explicit constants and practical byte estimates before proceeding. This is a
screening preference, not a claim that the user has set a specific byte cap.
An unspecified polynomial degree, large constant, growing field bit width or
unaccounted setup object does not clear this gate. Also account for ciphertext,
secret-key and shared-parameter size and polynomial honest computation costs.

Provide size formulas and illustrative byte estimates at target lambda values
128, 192 and 256 only when a candidate actually has a proposed parameter map.
Label these as encoding calculations at target strengths, not measured or
established security levels. Keep one-time setup costs separate from recurring
costs, while reporting both. Do not hide per-key cost by declaring setup global.
Publishing a setup witness or private word is not a valid compression of a
public representation. Representation changes require an explicit distribution
and explanation of what information is published.

Do not perform concerted attacks against candidates or their assumptions. This
overrides section 3 and the attack-experiment suggestions elsewhere above.
Retain candidate definitions and evidence; make constructive changes when they
have a stated purpose. Record security unknowns candidly. Literature comparison,
mathematical size accounting and honest-correctness reasoning remain in scope.
After a candidate clears the scalability gate, small correctness experiments
may be useful; passing them is not evidence of security. Until then, do not spend
effort on stress tests, parameter sweeps or proposal admission.

The existing nonlinear boundary-action proposal remains an unsubmitted research
record. Its measured toy costs and missing security-parameter mapping do not
clear this gate. Preserve it without further testing or submission while a new
design is considered. See kem-discovery/scalability-first-design-brief.txt for
the size analysis and the next design requirements.

Pipeline purpose and origin clarification (user steering, 2026-10-14)

The objective is to identify worthwhile research targets for Crucible's pipeline.
An impractical, incomplete or little-studied published proposal can merit an
entry. Community uptake and production readiness are not prerequisites for
research intake. A useful new construction or material variant can also merit
review without establishing an entirely new mathematical hardness family.

Keep three questions separate: whether the exact construction was previously
identified, whether its particular hardness assumption was previously identified,
and whether it is ready for implementation or live admission. Prior publication
remains prior art even when that proposal was unfinished. Conversely, reusing
an established agreement pattern does not settle the origin of a new concrete
action family, sampler and computational problem. Record the substantive delta
and its research value; routine packaging or a new name alone is insufficient.

Permit an explicitly incomplete research-intake record while security and
scalability remain unresolved. The earlier implementation/stress-test gate stays
in force; research intake does not authorize experiments, live admission or
submission. Novel construction proposals have provisional origin claims pending
review, with known mathematical and protocol predecessors credited. This
clarification supersedes any earlier interpretation requiring a completely new
hardness family merely to retain a candidate in the research pipeline.

Configuration and default-progression guidance (2026-10-16)

When a candidate has progressed far enough to describe parameters, distinguish
the admissible configuration space from a default progression for humans. A
configuration is an exact parameter vector or fully specified generation rule
within a reviewed domain, with stable identity. A stage is its ordinal on a
versioned default route; a challenge instance is a new random sample. Different
constructions need not have equal numbers of stages. Include useful natural
settings without changing mathematics for a count. A default-stage count in
multiples of ten, or five when appropriate, is a human presentation preference;
retain reviewed settings outside that default route. There is no fixed 50 target
or requirement of linear security spacing. Do not force an incomplete research
idea to provide a playable ladder.

Preserve the mathematical family, complete sampling distributions, KEM transform
and game. Coordinated changes in multiple parameters can be natural; justify
them. Do not engineer reachable stages using leaked information, specially easy
keys, padding, selectively accepted samples or artificially restricted randomness.
Native small spaces may permit enumeration; explain this without claiming that
small-instance attacks extrapolate to the proposed operating point.

Choose one strong common correctness envelope for admitted configurations. It can
be negligible and nonzero; exact probabilities need not coincide. Cover the full
claimed domain, disclose stronger individual bounds and material variation, and
report resource/generation aborts separately. Do not invent a bound or substitute
repeated successful trials for a correctness argument. Preserve a published or
approved reference setting where applicable; otherwise justify the candidate's
reference endpoint and mark unresolved security estimates as unresolved.

Explain cost tradeoffs under a declared workload, objective and candidate domain.
A limited search or favorable attack-model output is not global optimality or
security proof. Keep configuration identity, default route revision and security
evidence revision distinct; future findings must not rewrite earlier definitions.
The proposal's v1 wire field names tier_design, tier_meaning and supported_tiers
remain compatible. supported_tiers retains legacy numeric configuration selectors;
map default stage ordinals separately. Current ordinal points are provisional,
not a scientific objective for choosing parameters. Preserve existing configuration
credit if the human progression is curated or renumbered. See ladder-principles.txt for the accepted
contract. This guidance does not relax the earlier scalability gate, authorize
concerted attacks, or authorize experiments, submission or live admission.
