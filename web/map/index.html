How to read the map

Areas → families → schemes. Families collect related mathematical questions. Selecting a scheme reveals its specific security basis, nearby constructions, variants, and sources.

Families are a browsing model. Membership and distance do not establish security independence or a formal reduction. A scheme can have several relationships; this view gives it one primary home.

Notes underneath, map on top. Every scheme entry links to a descriptive note. Comparisons, improvements and procedural notes live alongside it, without becoming extra scheme cards. Literature notes document existing work and do not require a new-scheme proposal.

Mechanisms, development, analysis. Finding a public-key advantage, improving a construction, and testing its claims are different contributions. An entry can support all three; these are not maturity ranks.

KEMs only for now. Each entry identifies a KEM. Standalone encryption constructions and key-agreement directions are outside the active map.

All, Scored, Highlighted. All includes every mapped KEM. Scored includes schemes with an enrolled scoring release, using a snapshot of the current policy. Highlighted is our selected focus within Scored. Historical broken constructions are excluded from this map.

Published does not mean validated. Sources establish what a construction is. Current attack estimates, correctness, code, and natural stage progressions need separate, version-specific review. Local challenge releases may differ from the named published standard.
